A Private Fund Puts an AI Budget in the Offer: RMB 50k a Year, Unlimited Tokens

2026-09-24 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

The most unusual hiring terms I've seen this autumn recruiting season didn't come from a bulge bracket signing a new floor. They came from a small discretionary fund.

On September 22, He Li, general manager of Zhiyu Zhishan Investment (止于至善投资), publicly invited chief AI officers and AI application researchers to "jump into the bowl" — with a blunt slogan attached: unlimited tokens. The job posting gets specific: every new joiner immediately receives at least RMB 50,000 per year in discretionary AI usage credits, and applicants are asked to attach detailed records of how they actually use AI, plus representative cases.

Let's be clear about who this is. Zhiyu Zhishan is not a quant fund. It's a discretionary private fund with RMB 1–2 billion under management per AMAC registration. A small sample — but a genuinely new move: writing the cost of AI usage into the compensation structure of an offer is rare in publicly reported Chinese asset-management hiring.

The RMB 50k itself isn't the point. The point is that the pricing logic changed: AI capability is being treated as production capacity a company budget line has to buy — not an adjective on a resume.

The Quant Version: Splitting the Job Title

Quant funds solve the same problem differently. They split the job title.

In the same Shanghai Securities News roundup (Sep 22), Ubiquant (九坤投资) listed five distinct roles at its Peking University campus talk: quantitative strategy researcher, AI algorithm researcher, data scientist, quant implementation engineer, and AI engineer — the first three research-facing, the last two engineering-facing. Its experienced-hire board lists the AI algorithm researcher as the most urgent, working on machine-learning deployment across feature extraction, price forecasting and portfolio optimization. Minghong (明汯投资) opened a dedicated AI algorithm researcher track in its 2027 global campus recruiting; Kuande (宽德投资) is hiring machine learning researchers. On the mutual-fund side, E Fund set up a standalone "AI talent program", China Southern opened a fintech recruiting track, and Shenwan Lingxin (申万菱信) simply named the role "AI and Financial Engineering".

"Quant researcher" — five characters — is being split into a whole family of job titles. The division of labor is changing, and the grammar of hiring is changing with it.

The New York Version: Embed, or Buy

The same question, answered differently in New York.

Balyasny launched an AI fellowship in early September: no new job title, but AI specialists embedded into PM teams as "dedicated AI partners" — a few months with the central AI team first, then three-month rotations across trading groups. Per eFinancialCareers, the program targets master's candidates with three to five years of experience, with salaries up to $225,000. Balyasny runs roughly 20 AI researchers under Chief AI Officer Charlie Flanagan, who joined from Google in 2022; in a June OpenAI interview he disclosed that 97% of employees use the firm's internal AI platform daily, and that an economic analysis that took two days now takes 30 minutes.

Citadel's move was small but telling. eFinancialCareers reported that Alexey Poyarkov, who spent over a decade at TGS — a quant firm famous for how rarely people leave — has joined as head of a unit within its Global Quantitative Strategies group, after earlier stints at Google, Microsoft and Yandex. Sometimes platforms don't create a role for AI capability. They just buy the person.

What It Means for Candidates

Three different payment methods, one shared judgment: AI capability is moving from a bonus item to a diligence item.

  • Your AI track record is becoming diligence material. Zhiyu Zhishan asks for usage records; a Shanghai fund manager says they test "actual usage ability"; Balyasny prices its fellowship on three years of hands-on experience. No concrete AI case in your interview package is the new "no Python on your resume" of a decade ago.
  • Organization placement beats job title. Job titles will keep fragmenting. The more honest signal is where the role sits: an AI role embedded in an investment team usually sits closer to the decision loop than a standalone middle-office seat — Balyasny's approach is the one to watch.
  • An AI credit isn't a perk; it's trial-and-error capital. It's the company committing to fund your experiments. The budget structure tells you more about intent than the salary number does.

Does your firm write AI budgets into offers — or does it stay in the slogans? The direction, at least, is no longer in dispute: money is moving toward people who can actually use AI, from Beijing to New York. The only difference is how each firm pays — credits, job titles, or acquired veterans.
