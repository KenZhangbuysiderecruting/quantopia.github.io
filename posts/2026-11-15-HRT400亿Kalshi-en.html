HRT Joins $40 Billion Kalshi as the Third Wall Street Market Maker

2026-11-15 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

Three numbers first. $40 billion: Kalshi's valuation after its latest funding round. $1 billion: the size of that round. Third: HRT (Hudson River Trading) — following Jump Trading and Susquehanna, another Wall Street market maker has stepped onto prediction-market platform Kalshi. Per Bloomberg on November 6, HRT began quietly trading on Kalshi about a month ago, with the business run by its futures team. Three market makers have taken the same stage, none of them issuing a press release.

The move is measured. According to people familiar with the matter, HRT is currently participating in the platform's "Up-Down Markets" — contracts that settle on whether an asset's price rises or falls within a short window, mainly covering financial instruments. Sports and other non-financial event contracts remain untouched.

The boundary deserves a second look. Market makers' compliance frameworks were built for financial instruments; up-down contracts sit close to the short-horizon volatility business they already run, while sports books carry an entirely different risk and reputational profile. All three firms — Jump, Susquehanna and HRT — have so far kept their disclosed activity on the financial-contracts line. Where the compliance boundary sits, the business boundary follows.

Not Cheap Anymore

HRT is not only trading: it is also an investor in Kalshi's latest $1 billion round, which valued the platform at $40 billion. Holding a trading seat and an equity stake at the same time is an unusual combination for a market maker: earning the liquidity spread on one side, holding exposure to platform growth on the other.

Market makers are not the only ones circling. Crypto.com launched US crypto perpetuals last month (including a HYPE contract); Plus500's US futures commission merchant has begun offering Kalshi perpetual contracts; and Nasdaq-listed Hyperliquid Strategies filed on November 5 to establish an FCM that would bring Hyperliquid's perpetual markets to US clients. US perpetual-style products are expanding along several fronts at once.

Why Now

Prediction markets were filed under "alternative" two years ago; now three of the most risk-controlled market makers have entered in sequence. Two explanations fit. First, the short-cycle structure of up-down contracts mirrors a market maker's daily routine — frequent quoting, two-sided positions, fast hedging — so existing infrastructure can be reused directly. Second, event data itself is becoming a signal source: the pricing of financial event contracts embeds real-time expectations, and the closer to the trading desk, the more valuable it becomes.

For anyone recruiting inside quant firms, the signal is practical: the futures team's mandate is widening. When a firm hangs prediction-market activity under its futures desk, people who know event contracts, on-chain data and hedging tools gain an internal channel beyond the traditional futures seat.

Where Two Lines Cross

Kalshi is running event contracts; Jump has just built a compute-trading team covering OTC, perpetuals and prediction markets (Bloomberg, late October). The crossing point of those two lines — opening markets for new underlyings like compute and power inside a prediction market — may be where the next batch of quant roles comes from.
