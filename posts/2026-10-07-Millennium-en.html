Millennium's Four Hong Kong Roles: Quant JDs Now Demand LLM Skills

2026-10-07 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

In the first week of October, Millennium Management posted four types of quant roles across Hong Kong and Shanghai in quick succession. The most striking detail is not any single strategy — it is the JD for a Data Scientist role on a volatility team, which now lists LangGraph, LangSmith, vector databases and prompt engineering. "Knowing LLMs" on a quant research role is turning from a nice-to-have into an entry requirement.

Taken apart one by one, the four roles form a recruiting radar map of their own.

The first is a Quantitative Analyst role in Hong Kong, described as a "newly formed quantamental team" focused on systematic equity strategies, supporting semi-systematic trading in Japanese equities, led by a senior PM with more than ten years of alpha-mining experience. That is the signal worth noting most: a multi-strategy giant has started going "semi-systematic" in Asia — welding fundamental judgement to quant signals, rather than staying purely discretionary or purely black-box.

The second is a Volatility Researcher on the Volatility Alpha Development team, responsible for volatility surface fitting, options P&L datasets and event-driven volatility models, plus AI-based hypothesis testing. The bar is spelled out: a STEM master's or PhD, at least three years of investment management or quant research experience, Python/C++, with options and derivatives as hard knowledge.

The third is a Quantitative Researcher for Asia equities, covering four markets — China, Japan, Hong Kong and Taiwan — with a base to choose among Hong Kong, Shanghai, Singapore, Tokyo and Dubai. The JD repeatedly stresses "Asian market rules, trading conventions and venue characteristics". Writing factors is not enough; you need to know how specific markets match and settle trades.

The fourth is that Data Scientist role: beyond familiar names like PyTorch and JAX, the JD lists LangGraph for agentic research workflows, LangSmith for evaluation and observability, vector databases and embeddings for model evaluation, plus Kubernetes, Docker and Airflow. An entire LLM toolchain, written into a volatility research role.

Read together, the four roles reveal Millennium's recruiting logic in Asia: it is not hiring "general-purpose quants" but "quants with track depth" — Japanese equities, options volatility, Asian market rules. Each role asks you to know one specific market first, and talk factors second.

This forms a contrast with this season's campus recruiting on the mainland. Top domestic firms are competing for fresh graduates — internships advertised from RMB 60,000 a month, AI roles reported at up to RMB 2.5 million a year — while HR desks there also note an admission rate of about 0.5% and, in one account, fresh graduates' coding ability declining under AI reliance. Domestic firms are grabbing the "young + AI-fluent" base of the pyramid; foreign platforms in Hong Kong are grabbing the "experienced + market-fluent" top. The two are not the same pool of people at all.

But the two lines have started to cross, and the crossing point is the LLM toolchain: domestic firms write it into AI researcher JDs; foreign platforms write it into volatility researcher JDs. The difference is that the foreign desks want hybrids — people who hold hard market knowledge such as options and Japanese equities and can use LLMs as a productivity tool — not AI engineers who can only call APIs.

Zoom out further: on October 1, Millennium closed a $20bn new fund against demand reported above $30bn, taking AUM close to a hundred billion. With the money in place, the first move is laying people across Asia: in Hong Kong universities' 2027 campus recruiting, Millennium's postings include quantitative researcher, quant developer and AI intern; industry BD information suggests the Sydney team has also grown to about 20 people.

For practitioners, what this recruiting radar reveals is specific: as foreign multi-strategy platforms expand in Asia, what they lack most in the short term is not generalist AI talent, but people who know Japanese equities, options and Asian market rules — and can use the AI toolchain. The former decides whether you get in; the latter decides whether you stay.

Has your team started writing the LLM toolchain into its JDs yet?
