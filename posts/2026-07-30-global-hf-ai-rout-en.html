While Global Hedge Funds Hit a Record $5.6 Trillion, AI Quant Strategies Suffer Their Biggest Drawdown Since February 2024

2026-07-30 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

On July 28, 2026, two numbers appeared on my screen at the same time. On the left: Man Group published its half-year results — AUM reached $253.6 billion, an all-time high since the company went public. CEO Robyn Grew said: "Clients are consolidating their capital with fewer, larger partners." On the right: Bloomberg, citing people familiar with the matter, reported that the Asia-focused WT China Fund fell roughly 17% in the first three weeks of July; Keystone Investors fell about 12%. And just in the first half of this year, these two funds were up roughly 120% and 63%, respectively. Same day, same industry. Heaven and hell, it turns out, are only four weeks apart.

1. The First-Half Party: The Best Quarter Ever, the Most Expensive Cake

The hedge fund industry delivered a record-breaking first half of 2026.

Man Group AUM: $253.6B — highest since listing (half-year results, July 28)

Industry total AUM: $5.6T — up $409.3B in a single quarter (HFR, July 24)

Q2 industry return: +12.3% — best quarter in a decade (Citco, July 24)

H1 average return: +7.4% — best half-year since 2009 (PivotalPath)

Q2 net inflows: $39.2B — highest on record (Citco)

This isn't a "recovery" — it's a downpour. Singapore's GIC and the Abu Dhabi Investment Council plan to allocate tens of billions of dollars to the hedge fund industry. Man Group's long-term strategies saw net inflows of $7.1 billion — more than 5x analysts' expectations.

But here's the problem — the cake is getting bigger, but fewer are sharing it. Grew's "consolidating relationships with fewer, larger firms" is not a platitude. It's a verdict on the industry: mid-sized hedge funds are being pushed off the table.

2. July's Abrupt Turn: When AI Conviction Meets a Stampede

Then, in July, the music stopped.

HedgeWeek's July 28 report shows Asian hedge funds hitting the brakes after a first-half surge. WT China Fund was up roughly 120% in the first half, but gave back about 17% in the first three weeks of July. Keystone Investors swung from +63% to -12%. FengHe Asia, CloudAlpha and Indus Capital Partners all pulled back across the board.

SK hynix: down roughly 28% in July

Kioxia: down roughly 28% in July, about 40% off its peak

South Korea's KOSPI: plunged nearly 40% from its June 22 high

Morgan Stanley prime data (July 21): volatility in crowded AI positions re-emerged, momentum strategies reversed

A J.P. Morgan report on July 29 estimates Korean hedge funds have deleveraged about 90% of the way, but at what cost? Net-to-gross dropped from a peak of 5.7x to 3.2x. What was once deemed the "can't-miss trade" has become the "position everyone wants to escape".

3. The China Mirror: Alpha Down from 14% to 3%, Quants Forced to "Buy Themselves"

Turn the lens to China, and the mirror image is startlingly clear.

High-Flyer (幻方量化) CSI 1000 index-enhanced strategy (week ending July 17): -15.7%

Hande Investment (涵德投资) CSI 1000 strategy (same period): -16.1%

CSI 1000 index (July 1–24): -20.59%

Average drawdown of quant long strategies that week: -10.06% vs -6.39% for discretionary long

Average H1 excess return of 1,236 index-enhanced products: 3.11% (vs 14.17% a year earlier)

Share of quant stock-selection products with positive YTD returns: only 31.93% (167 of 523)

These figures come from PaiPaiWang (私募排排网) — third-party data, not media estimates. Excess return falling from 14.17% to 3.11%, a drop of over 10 percentage points within a year, is not normal volatility. This is the collective failure of strategy models under an extreme style rotation.

In a letter to investors, Hande Investment used a phrase: "盈亏同源" — gains and losses share the same source. In plain terms: the money made on momentum factors is now being given back the same way. Momentum, liquidity and short-term reversal — factors that usually offset each other — all crashed in the same direction in July.

Then something more interesting happened: Lingjun Investment (灵均投资) announced RMB 200 million of self-purchases, followed by Pingfanghe Investment (平方和投资) with RMB 100 million. As of July 21, private funds' YTD self-purchases totaled RMB 858 million, of which July accounted for 88.07%. The equity private fund positioning index hit 83.70% — a four-year high.

On one side, single-week drops of 15%; on the other, self-purchases and raising exposure in parallel. Put the two signals together and readers can draw their own conclusions.

4. Bridgewater's Quiet Moment: Human's 8.1% vs Machine's 8.1%

Meanwhile, on a different narrative track, Bridgewater produced a data point most people missed.

In the first half of 2026, Bridgewater's flagship Pure Alpha macro fund returned 8.1%. At the same time, the firm's AI-driven macro strategy — the AIA Macro fund — also returned 8.1%. Exactly identical.

A belief system Ray Dalio built over 50 years and a machine algorithm produced exactly the same result through six months of wars, tariffs and a tech bubble. Since AIA Macro launched in late 2023, its annualized return has exceeded 11%, and it now manages several billion dollars.

For mid-sized hedge funds considering cutting their quant teams and shifting to arbitrage strategies, this data point may be more instructive than Man Group's $253.6 billion.

5. A Recruiter's View: Who's Hiring, Who's Cutting

The impact of this "binary split" on the talent market is already an open card.

The winners are hiring: Man Group is expanding its North America team and accelerating its AI transformation; Point72 hired a weather-forecasting specialist from Citadel for an AI prediction role (July 27, Alternative Fund Insight); Bridgewater's AI team keeps growing. Top platforms are buying the future of "every strategy + AI infrastructure".

The losers are cutting: when quant factors fail en masse, models must be rewritten — but rewriting takes time, and investors won't wait. Some mid-sized quant private funds are crossing the line from "self-purchasing" to "being redeemed". From a recruiter's perspective, the quant talent market in the second half of 2026 will present an interesting picture: top quant firms are still fighting over talent (especially researchers in AI/ML), while hiring demand at smaller and mid-sized quant platforms will contract structurally.

For candidates, the key question is no longer "can I get into quant" but "which kind of quant should I join".
