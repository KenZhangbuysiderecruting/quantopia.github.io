A $35B Bond Fund Launches a Quant Commodity CTA, Led by an AQR Helix Veteran

2026-09-13 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

Quant CTAs are not a new species. But when a firm managing roughly $35 billion — built on fixed-income and currency relative value — carves out a dedicated commodity fund for a former AQR systematic strategist, it is worth a second look.

A fund filed back in July

The fund is called Capula Alternative Markets Alpha Fund. An SEC Form D shows it was filed in July 2026. Bloomberg and HedgeWeek reported on September 1 that the strategy is a quantitative CTA (commodity trading advisor) with a target capacity of $2–3 billion.

Capula and AQR both declined to comment. The capacity figure comes from people familiar with the matter — not an official commitment, and not money already raised.

Who is running it

The head is Yao Hua Ooi. He joined Capula from AQR in 2022, after roughly 20 years at AQR, where he was a principal and worked on the firm's Helix systematic strategy, a book of about $6.3 billion.

Helix sits in a less-publicised corner of AQR — systematic multi-asset trend and relative value, distinct from the style-factor and equity-quant work AQR is better known for. Reaching principal on that line means a specific skill: finding signal where there is no clean factor structure.

Commodities are exactly that kind of place. No P/E, no coupon, no central bank anchoring the price. The drivers are inventories, freight, weather and geopolitics. Port a stock-quant framework over and it mostly will not work.

Why an old house changes lane

Capula's core competence is fixed-income and currency relative value — harvesting mispricings, which requires enough of them to exist and low enough friction to capture them.

That premise has been loosening. Rate paths have converged and major-currency volatility has compressed, leaving fewer deviations to capture. It is not that the trade stopped working; the alpha per unit of team has thinned.

Commodities run on a different logic, driven by non-financial variables and largely uncorrelated with rates and FX. For a fixed-income house it is a genuinely new asset — not because returns are higher, but because it does not overlap with the existing book.

So this looks less like chasing a trend and more like filling a gap.

Two other moves in the same period

Stretch the timeline and this launch is not isolated. In 2025 Capula hired John Anderson — former co-head of global fixed income and commodities at Millennium — to build a multi-strategy platform. In 2026 it allocated $450 million to external manager Cinctive Capital.

Read together: hire someone who has built a multi-strategy platform, give money to an external manager, then launch a new strategy fund of your own. That is not "adding a strategy." That is platformisation — moving from a focused boutique toward a multi-strategy platform.

What it means for commodity CTA talent in China

Domestic commodity-futures quant in China has become crowded: strategy homogenisation, capacity limits, and fee and slippage erosion pushing teams toward multi-asset and multi-horizon approaches.

Overseas the path is being walked in reverse — from fixed income into commodities, from relative value into trend. Different directions, same problem: single-asset-class alpha increasingly struggles to support a full platform.

That reshapes hiring. Chinese commodity-CTA searches typically want futures high-frequency and multi-horizon cross-sectional skill. These new overseas funds want someone who can combine trend, carry and relative value into one book and manage cross-asset risk. The two skill sets overlap less than people assume; a cross-market move usually needs six to twelve months to close the gap.

A question worth asking: does your commodity team go deep on a single complex, or move toward multi-asset portfolios? The capability requirements diverge quickly.

One line to close

A $2–3 billion fund is not big news in global hedge funds. But the signal is clear: when a relative-value house starts buying trend exposure, the room for single-strategy business shrinks another notch.

Yao Hua Ooi took four years to travel from AQR to Capula. Whether the fund performs is a separate question — the direction is already set.
