CSI 1000 Fell 8.5%, and Its Enhanced Index Funds Still Beat It by 0.85%

2026-11-04 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

Last week, 26 to 30 October, CSI 1000 fell 8.51% and CSI 500 fell 8.05%. Over the same five sessions, enhanced index funds tracking CSI 1000 averaged +0.85% excess return, and those tracking CSI 500 averaged +0.88%.

CSI 300 fell only 2.92%. Enhanced products tracking CSI 300 averaged -0.47%.

The index that fell hardest produced positive alpha. The index that fell least produced negative alpha. For one week, the beta and the alpha ran on separate tracks.

RMB 107.1bn, and nearly half of it went into one pool.

Listed ETFs took in RMB 110.09bn net last week. Equity ETFs accounted for 108.45bn, and broad-based ETFs 107.13bn. Broken down: CSI 300 ETFs +50.50bn, CSI A500 ETFs +23.91bn, ChiNext ETFs +14.77bn, STAR 50 ETFs +12.61bn, ChiNext 50 ETFs +3.06bn.

The CSI 300 complex alone absorbed 47.1% of all broad-based inflows, dividing 50.50 by 107.13.

"Buy the dip" was only half true last week. Broad-based products did absorb money, but the money was not spread evenly.

Sector and thematic ETFs went the other way.

Thematic sector ETFs saw RMB 4.10bn of net outflows. Technology -8.19bn, cyclicals -4.22bn, consumer -0.72bn, healthcare -0.01bn; financials and real estate +6.78bn, advanced manufacturing +2.29bn.

The mutual fund tape agrees: financial and property theme funds returned a median +2.48% last week, TMT theme funds -11.54%, manufacturing theme funds -4.59%.

So the money did not sweep the whole market. It moved house. Over the same week cross-border ETFs bled RMB 3.88bn and commodity ETFs RMB 0.77bn. Money leaving and money arriving were not heading in the same direction.

Why was alpha negative in the pool that took the most money?

One explanation: heavy subscriptions into CSI 300 ETFs lift the constituents through passive buying, compressing the relative price gaps between them, which narrows the space a stock-selection model can work in. CSI 500 and CSI 1000 have no comparable passive bid, and their internal dispersion is higher, so the source of excess return is still there.

That is an inference, not a finding. The research note published three excess-return numbers and no attribution. Two things would test it: whether CSI 300 enhanced products post negative excess a second week running, and whether dispersion inside the CSI 300 narrows at the same time. If both hold, the explanation stands.

So did quant managers make or lose money last week?

Quant equity funds averaged -5.4%. Market-neutral funds -0.91%. Active quant funds posted a median -6.44%, and a one-year median of -9.77%.

Plus 0.88% does not offset minus 8.05%. Excess return is relative; NAV is absolute. Both numbers can be true at once. Show the client the excess, look at the NAV yourself — the same weekly report reads two ways.

Has the trading environment come back? Half an answer.

Ten-day average turnover was about RMB 1.54trn, against 1.52trn the week before, still near the low end of the year. The median turnover rate was 1.78%, essentially flat.

Cross-sectional dispersion rose from 2.75% to 2.79%, its third consecutive weekly gain. Dispersion is the fuel for alpha, but 2.79% is still a low absolute level. The top 10% of stocks by turnover accounted for 56.3% of volume, unchanged, so concentration at the top has not loosened.

Three weeks of recovery is three steps out of a hole, not a return to level ground.

The "new crowding" in style, noted for the record.

The CSI Dividend index rose 3.6% last week, the only major index to close higher, taking its year-to-date return back to +1.6%. Among the 31 Shenwan level-one sectors, banks gained 4.6%, food and beverage 3.0%, transport 2.1%; telecoms fell 15.1% and electronics 14.2%.

At the factor level, the highest-value bucket fell 2.7%, the smallest decline among 30 buckets across six factor families. Low-volatility and low-turnover factors have now led for close to a month.

Money has been gathering in defensive exposure for a month. Whether that counts as crowding needs two more weeks — crowding is not defined by rising together, but by refusing to fall together and then getting crowded together.

Three things to check next week.

One, whether CSI 500 and CSI 1000 enhanced funds post positive excess for a second week. Two, whether cross-sectional dispersion clears 3%. Three, whether broad-based inflows spread from CSI 300 into CSI 500 and CSI 1000 — if they do, the same force will compress the excess.

If all three hold, this week's alpha came from the environment. If only one does, it came from the models. That distinction matters, and right now it cannot be settled.
