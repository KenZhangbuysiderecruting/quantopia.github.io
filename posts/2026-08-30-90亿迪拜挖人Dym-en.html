$9B, Dubai, and Talent Raids: How Dymon Is Mapping a Three-Continent Franchise with Middle East Capital and Rivals' People

2026-08-30 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

Bloomberg ran its story on Thursday evening and the macro community is buzzing again. Marwan Moubachir — who previously ran global risk across Schonfeld's discretionary macro and fixed income business — is set to join Dymon Asia Capital's Dubai office as Managing Director early next year. He is the third person Dymon has "moved out" of the same Schonfeld team over the past two months. It sounds like an ordinary poach. But once you lay the numbers on the table, the picture gets more complicated. Dymon's assets under management stood at roughly $9 billion at the end of July — nearly double the $5 billion target it publicly stated at the start of the year. The multi-strategy fund is up a net +7.5% year-to-date. The Abu Dhabi Investment Council (ADIC) has committed around $1 billion in tranches. How did a Singapore fund founded over a decade ago suddenly take on the outlines of a "global player"?

First, Let's Unpack the "Poaching"

Moubachir is not an isolated event. Piecing together public information, the recent hiring list has three names.

Manas Baveja, formerly a macro trader at Schonfeld, recently joined Dymon in London as a macro trader. Marwan Moubachir, formerly global head of risk for Schonfeld's discretionary macro and fixed income business, moves into the Dubai office as Managing Director early next year. Amir Ravan, formerly head of commodities at Jain Global, joins as head of commodities. Jay Radia, formerly COO of commodities at BlueCrest, joined around the same time.

Look at that list and two features stand out. First, three of the new hires come from Schonfeld's macro and commodities desks — the same firm, the same desks, the same network of former bosses. This kind of "cluster poaching" is rare in cross-region, cross-license hedge funds. It looks less like backfilling a gap and more like a systematic talent build-out.

Second, the destinations are all different — London, Dubai, Singapore. Behind each location sits a product line. The stated purpose of Moubachir's hire is to expand the macro and relative value business, reporting to Kenneth Kan (senior partner) and working alongside the existing Omar Itani. String it all together, and Dymon is assembling a jigsaw that can run macro and commodities across three time zones spanning Asia, Europe and the Middle East.

The Money Story Matters More Than the People

The money side usually gets buried under the talent headlines, but this time the money is the foundation. Dymon's size has jumped sharply in recent months — roughly $9 billion at the end of July, against a public year-end target of $5 billion. The core source of that jump is ADIC's allocation. ADIC, an Abu Dhabi sovereign-fund-backed investor, has committed roughly $1 billion to Dymon in tranches. ADIC has also said publicly that it plans to build its overall hedge fund exposure to around $15 billion.

Once $1 billion of "sovereign money" lands in a Singapore fund's pocket, the source of capital changes — and so does the way that capital gets deployed. In practice, Dymon's spending shows up in two directions: first, placing people in Dubai and London to build out product capability across the Middle East and Central European time zones; second, standing up a commodities team, because commodities and macro sit together naturally.

When the capital side and the talent side move at the same time, it is usually the hallmark of a phase-shift "expansion window" in a hedge fund. The multi-strategy fund's +7.5% year-to-date return is the key backdrop — LPs allocate because performance is strong, and strong performance in turn lets the fund use bonus dollars to hire more aggressively.

Key numbers: AUM ~$9.0B (as of July 2026); start-of-year AUM target ~$5.0B; multi-strategy fund YTD ~+7.5%; ADIC committed allocation ~$1.0B (in tranches); ADIC plans ~$15B hedge fund exposure. The Singapore headquarters is expanding in parallel into Dubai and London.

Why Singapore Has Become the "Midfield"

Singapore has long been seen as the natural home base for Asian hedge funds — sensible on tax, regulation, talent visas, and flight times from Hong Kong and Tokyo. But Dymon's expansion path seems to say something else: Singapore is no longer just an "Asia HQ." It is the "midfield" of a franchise that spans Asia, Europe and the Middle East.

Put people in Dubai with ADIC's money behind them, and you cover Middle East clients and liquidity; put them in London and you get European clients, European regulation and European-session liquidity. The Asia team runs Asia-Pacific markets. That is a three-location architecture. If Moubachir actually builds out the relative value (RV) business, Dymon gains a "global macro + RV" leg alongside its multi-strategy platform.

By comparison, Dymon has not hired into New York at scale. That may be deliberate — New York is costly, its tax treatment is complicated, and macro and RV, unlike long/short equities, do not depend on the US market-making ecosystem. The choice of where you hire reveals the product positioning in reverse.

What It Means for Practitioners

This is the part I want to flag as a recruiter. Macro and RV talent is structurally scarce right now.

Over the past two years, macro strategies have kept recovering in global LP allocations — Bridgewater's AIA Macro, Brevan Howard's restructured pure-macro products, and established names like Andurand keep attracting new money. The result is a broad intensification of the fight for global heads of risk, macro PMs and commodities traders. The demand signal for someone at Moubachir's level is unambiguous. Macro talent that came out of a "prop-trading heritage" shop like Schonfeld is especially in demand, because their training is closer to raw discipline than buy-side academia.

If you work in macro or RV, expect to see more moves like Dymon's over the next 12 months. If you are considering a move to a multi-strategy platform, watch the Singapore–Dubai–London triangle closely — Dubai in particular, where several top institutions have already set up over the past 12 months. A Singapore fund opening an MD seat in Dubai only tightens an already tight market.
