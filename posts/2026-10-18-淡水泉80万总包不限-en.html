Danshuiquan's RMB 800K Packages, All Majors Welcome: Did the Bar Drop?

2026-10-18 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

Danshuiquan wrote "all majors welcome" into its campus recruiting. A discretionary manager advertising in the language of a quant shop is not a lower bar — it is a different ruler.

The numbers first

Per Daily Economic News on Oct 15, since August the recruiting teams of Danshuiquan, Ubiquant, High-Flyer and Minghong have been running through Tsinghua, Peking University and other campuses — earlier in the season, and with more seats, than in prior years.

Item2026 campus range
Pre-tax monthly base, core researchRMB 35,000 – 50,000
Master's total package (incl. variable bonus)RMB 500,000 – 800,000
Top PhDs with competition or research pedigreeHigher; no disclosed ceiling
Intern day rateRMB 800 – 1,500

The figures themselves are not new — Chinese quant funds have paid top campus rates for years. What deserves attention is where "all majors" comes from: Danshuiquan's Alpha Pioneer programme, which explicitly breaks with the discretionary side's long-standing "finance majors only" habit.

All majors, with a condition attached

The second half of that sentence reads: science or engineering background required, an appetite for working with models, probability and algorithms, and an interest in exploring where discretionary judgment and quantitative methods meet.

Translated: nobody will look at your diploma. They will look at whether you can speak in mathematics.

That is not looser screening. It is a different variable. Screens used to ask what you studied; now they ask what you can compute. At the top end the boundary between finance, maths and computer science has blurred to the point of irrelevance — the one thing that stays sharp is quantitative ability. The report is direct about this: maths, physics, computer science and financial engineering are the closest fits, a master's is broadly the floor, and competition maths, modelling or machine-learning projects are the plus marks.

Three signals in the same room

Ubiquant's Wutong programme for the 2027 cohort is under way, covering quant strategy, AI algorithms, data science, plus a long list of development and risk seats. One 2025 campus hire cited in the report came from Tsinghua's Institute for Interdisciplinary Information Sciences — the Yao class. Top-of-market offers still go to a very narrow band of candidates.

High-Flyer, which has posted strategy and deep-learning roles, said outright that candidates without quant internships can still get written tests and interviews if they are strong enough. Internship experience has been demoted from hard filter to plus mark — a rare concession in a campus JD.

Wanyan's Rolling Stone programme puts newcomers through the full path of factor research, backtesting and live deployment alongside the team. What it offers is research soil, not a title.

Put these side by side and one line appears: the top firms are widening the entrance while keeping the ruler hard. Wider gate — all majors, no internship required. Harder ruler — quantitative ability, modelling, practical use of AI tools.

A head of quant at one of these firms put the second half better than any framework: AI has solved the general efficiency problem, and people who can ask good questions, with depth of judgement, have become scarcer.

Put RMB 800,000 on a world map

Kadoa's October salary board puts top-tier US quant researcher base for PhDs at USD 235,000 – 300,000 (this publication analysed that board on Oct 4). A top Chinese package of RMB 800,000 is roughly USD 110,000 — a gap of close to two times.

But look at the structure rather than the gap. The US number is a narrow funnel that starts at PhD. The Chinese number this season is a wide funnel that a master's graduate can reach. The two "tops" are not screening the same population.

Two practical conclusions

First, the name of your degree is no longer the ticket. Non-finance candidates with solid quantitative grounding and modelling or ML projects are, for the first time, formally invited in by a discretionary manager.

Second, practical AI-tool usage has moved from resume decoration to interview subject — and the question is not whether you have used it, but how you balance it against your own domain knowledge.
