Five Rings Tops the League at $269K: Has Quant Pay Really Flipped?

2026-10-04 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

One detail in Kadoa's October salary table reverses the narrative of the past six months: machine learning engineers (MLE) show a median base of $225,000, ahead of quantitative researchers (QR) at $205,000 — for a second consecutive month.

This is not a one-month wobble. MLE was already ahead of QR in September, and October confirms it. Five Rings Capital topped the disclosed list with a median base of $269,000.

The scope matters. Kadoa's data comes from 4,294 open quant roles, of which 1,059 disclose base pay, with a median of $188,000. It covers only roles that publish a number — not a full industry compensation survey; senior bonuses and PM profit shares are not included. Its value is precisely that it sets the entry price: what each role pays at the bottom for someone entering or making a lateral move.

Split by role, October ranks like this:

MLE $225K | HFT $213K | QR $205K | QD $203K | SWE $200K | Data Scientist $188K | Quant Trader $188K

Two years ago, MLE ahead of QR would have been treated as noise. Quantitative researchers were traditionally the top of the pay pyramid, with MLE seen as a support role paid a notch below research. Two months of reversal suggests employers are now applying a systematic premium to people who can put ML models into the trading pipeline.

Five Rings' $269,000 top spot is more telling still. It is not a diversified platform; it is a firm focused on proprietary quant trading, and its median base outranks names like Citadel, HRT and Jane Street. That does not necessarily mean Five Rings pays the most in total — many giants do not disclose bonuses — but it sends a signal: small-sample, high-concentration prop shops are using transparent base pay to compete for people at the ML-engineering intersection.

The same week brought several corroborating data points.

A Citadel Quantitative Research Engineer — PhD Graduate role posted on Alion on October 2 carries a base range of $235,000 to $300,000. IMC's Graduate Quantitative Trader role lists a $250,000 base. Tradermath's compilation shows Jane Street, HRT, SIG and Jump Trading starting fresh graduates at $300,000 base, with traders, researchers and software engineers all at the same starting level.

Put together, three lines appear at once.

First, ML/AI skills are gaining pricing power in quant roles. Not "can write TensorFlow", but being able to move an ML model from a research environment into production trading and handle overfitting and out-of-sample decay.

Second, the tail of base pay is rising. Five years ago a $200,000 base was the top of the market for fresh graduates; now $250,000 to $300,000 is the normal range at first-tier platforms. That lifts the anchor for the whole talent market — even where mid-tier firms cannot follow, candidates' expectations have been recalibrated.

Third, the buy side has more competitors. Recruiting from Scratch's September data shows 195 open engineering and quant roles at New York hedge funds and trading firms, led by Point72, Jane Street and Two Sigma — but AI labs are also opening New York roles for the same people. Candidates are no longer only comparing fund offers against fund offers; they are comparing a quant offer against an AI lab offer.

In my years recruiting, one habit when reading pay data: don't look at the average. Look at who is setting the new anchor.

This time the new anchor is being set not by traditional hedge fund research roles but by ML engineers, low-latency systems engineers, and people who can wire models into trading infrastructure. Five Rings' $269K, Citadel's $235K-$300K and IMC's $250K are all repricing that skill set.

For anyone looking at opportunities, the conclusion is direct: if your skills sit between pure research and pure engineering, this is the window with the highest premium; if you only do one end, the next six months may squeeze you harder.
