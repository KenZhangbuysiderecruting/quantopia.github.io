Point72's Four QR Postings: Same Title, Three Kinds of Authority

2026-11-17 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

Same title, same update date, three different mandates. Cubist Systematic Strategies, the systematic-trading affiliate of Point72, has a Quantitative Researcher posting in New York, Hong Kong and Singapore at the same time. New York asks for "independently conducting quantitative finance research", 3 to 7 years. Hong Kong asks for "responsible for some or all of parts of the research pipeline", 2+ years. Singapore opens with "Entry-Level" and follows with "You will be trained in all aspects of systematic trading".

Three postings, three verbs: choose, hold, be trained

Four dimensions, side by side:

1. Positioning: New York runs independent research. Hong Kong owns some or all of a research pipeline. Singapore is an entry-level seat where you get trained first.

2. Experience bar: New York 3–7 years. Hong Kong 2+ years. Singapore does not require prior finance experience.

3. Responsibility verbs: New York — select, independently. Hong Kong — maintain, improve. Singapore — assist, be trained.

4. Asset scope: New York lists equities, futures, fixed income, credit and FX. Hong Kong is global equities with mid-frequency as a plus. Singapore is unscoped.

New York: this person picks the methodology

The first column deserves a pause. The New York posting contains the phrase "methodology selection" — the methodology is chosen by this hire. That is not "what to work on"; it is "which method to work with", the earliest link in the research chain. The same sentence includes performance monitoring: topic selection through post-launch monitoring, one uninterrupted stretch.

The Hong Kong posting never claims methodology selection. It describes a pipeline — data processing, feature design, model training, portfolio construction, backtesting, performance analysis — "some or all of parts". Then comes a sentence that does not read like recruiting copy: "maintaining and improving portfolio trading in production environments". That sentence buys ownership, not breadth. One stretch of the pipeline is yours, including the part where it runs on real money and breaks on your watch.

Singapore is the most direct. Entry level, will be trained. "Successful hires will ultimately become thought leaders" is the promise; "Follow, digest and analyze the latest academic research" is the daily work. Another duty: building analytical tools for a shared research framework.

Hong Kong is the only city mapped to two templates

One detail is easy to misread. Hong Kong actually carries two QR postings: the independent seat above, and a second one inside a seven-city combined requisition (Dubai, London, Paris, Hong Kong, Sydney, Toronto, Tokyo) whose copy is word-for-word identical to the Singapore posting — entry-level. So Hong Kong is not being downgraded; it is holding both ends — seasoned hands to keep production running, and a seat in the entry pool. Across these four postings, Hong Kong is the only city that maps to two different templates at once.

Swapping the platform variable: DRW, New York and Singapore

Now swap the multi-strategy-platform variable for a proprietary trading firm. DRW, New York and Singapore.

The New York Quantitative Researcher sits under an "expanding Mid-Frequency Systematic Trading team", and the first responsibility is to "Apply statistical and machine learning methods across diverse datasets to build trading models that capture novel signals". The New York Quantitative Developer posting is blunter: "We are building a new systematic, mid-frequency trading business at DRW", self-described as an "early-stage effort within an established trading firm".

In Singapore, both technical seats hang off the same product line. The Quantitative Developer designs, builds and improves pricing and execution systems for the Asia Equity Options business, "as a member of the desk". The low-latency engineering role builds execution for the same line, sitting between research and trading. The research posting includes "Assist software developers to translate research strategies into production software" — assist, not own.

Same firm: New York is building a new business; Singapore is deepening an established one. This is not a ranking of ability. It is two stages of the same money — one stage needs someone to define what gets built, the other needs someone to make it stable, fast and scalable.

Circle the verbs, skip the adjectives

To locate decision authority, ignore the title and read the verbs. Three signals:

Where you see select, design, lead, own the research agenda — that is the definition end.

Where you see maintain, improve, support, in production — that is the holding end: real P&L-adjacent responsibility, inside boundaries someone else has drawn.

Where you see assist, be trained, contribute to a shared framework — that is the capacity end.

All three signals often sit inside one firm, under one title, split across cities.

For candidates: same title, different negotiation logic

The practical effect is not "which city to pick" but that the same title carries different negotiation logic. Definition-end seats are negotiated on research record and judgment. Holding-end seats are negotiated on whether you can take over a system that already runs — the friction is usually process and language, not modelling. Capacity-end seats are the widest door, but the JD says "be trained" out loud: the first two years are a learning period, and that is the JD's own wording, not subtext.

Three questions are enough in an interview: who gets the final call on this method? whose evaluation framework does my output feed? is the growth path horizontal (more asset classes) or vertical (leading people)?

For candidates from mainland China, the three doors are not equally wide. Definition-end seats generally require an overseas research record, or a PhD with verifiable live results. Holding-end seats hinge on taking over a running system. Capacity-end seats are the most accessible, with the training period written in plain text.

The limit: a JD is copy, not an org chart

One boundary to state clearly. A JD is recruiting copy, not an organization chart. It tells you what the firm wants this person to do; it cannot tell you who actually decides. Three postings may come from different recruiters or different template vintages — these four QR postings happened to be updated on the same day (9 November), which makes the comparison a bit more reliable, but it remains evidence at the copy level.

Next time you open a JD, skip the adjectives in the responsibilities section and circle the verbs. Three circled verbs, and the seat's place in the organization is basically out.
