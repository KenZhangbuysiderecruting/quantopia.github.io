Citadel's 2-Year Non-Compete: Zero Days in California

2026-11-22 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

The same clause that holds a person for two years in New York holds nobody for a single day in California. Citadel just put a new equities quant unit there.

The unit is GQS SCI, Southern California Investments, based in Irvine. Bloomberg's reporting is specific about the strategy: short-horizon systematic equities, the Jane Street and Hudson River Trading end of the spectrum rather than multi-factor books held for weeks.

Hedgeweek carried the Bloomberg report on 19–20 November. A second move landed alongside it: Citadel hired Thomas Lefebvre, who ran global commodities options at Hartree Partners, as a partner and portfolio manager for global volatility trading in London. His appointment is verifiable on the UK FCA register and in Companies House records.

This is not the October story again

At the end of September Bloomberg reported that Citadel's roughly 180-person quant group was expanding, targeting "healthy double-digit" growth over a year, and named AI labs as a recruiting source. The same reporting mentioned that Poyarkov had arrived from TGS to lead a new team — without a name and without a city.

This round adds three things: the unit has a name, an address, and a legal constraint it cannot argue with. The earlier story was about where the people come from. This one is about how they are held. The second question carries the actual information.

What a non-compete actually sells

Not loyalty. Time.

A two-year clause means a competitor gets the person — and whatever is in his head but not yet in a document — two years later. For short-horizon strategies that time has a very concrete value: signal half-life runs at roughly 18 months, so by the time the clause expires the edge has largely expired with it.

That is why the clause works as a moat in most jurisdictions and not at all in California. The state has tightened steadily: §16600 voids any contract restraining a lawful profession, SB 699 makes attempting to enforce one unlawful regardless of where or when it was signed, and AB 1076 makes merely including one in an employment contract a civil violation. The exceptions left are corporate — sale of a business, dissolution of a partnership — not employment.

So what holds people instead

Deferred compensation. Bloomberg's phrasing is that it "could become more important." The industry phrasing is blunter: if the contract cannot stop someone, the money not yet paid out can.

For anyone doing the hiring, that is structural. An offer no longer competes only against current annual cash; it has to cover the deferred balance someone forfeits by moving. That number rarely appears on the offer letter, and it usually decides whether the person moves at all.

If SCI scales, the nearest pool is TGS in the same city: the head of the unit came from there, and nobody has to relocate. The cost is equally clear — the thicker TGS's deferred book, the higher the price of hiring out of it. The report's line about needing substantial packages to offset forfeited deferred pay describes exactly that mechanism.

Why Asia feels this

Non-competes in Hong Kong and Singapore are litigable. There are fixed terms, garden leave, and case law behind them.

So an offer in Asia buys a person plus a period of time; an offer in California buys only the person.

Same firm, same job level, two different pricing logics: Asia pays for the certainty that someone will not walk into a competitor next month; California does not pay that, but pays it back in deferral. Cross-border hiring routinely blends the two cost lines, and the usual mistake is comparing first-year cash only.

What is inference, stated as inference

Bloomberg does not say how many people SCI has now, or how many it intends to hire. The firm's site says only that the unit was founded in 2026 and uses GQS execution and optimisation tools to bring ideas to market quickly. Headcount and hiring pace in Irvine are not public.

Recruiting from TGS is framed in the original reporting as a possibility — proximity plus a former employer — not as something that has happened. The Lefebvre hire is confirmed: the FCA register and Companies House both carry it, which is why it belongs in the same paragraph as SCI.

The Wellington +15% figure is eFinancialCareers' number and runs only through the end of October.

One thing worth taking away: put a California offer on the table and the question is not what the non-compete says — it will say nothing enforceable. The question is what sits unvested three years out.
