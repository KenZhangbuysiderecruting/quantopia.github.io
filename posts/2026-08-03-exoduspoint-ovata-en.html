Hong Kong's $1.5B Fund Ovata Shut Down: Asia Equity Talent Flocks to Platforms

2026-08-03 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

In 2024, Hong Kong hedge fund Segantii Capital Management shut down, triggering a scramble for Asian trading talent. Two years later, the same script is playing out at another Hong Kong fund. Ovata Capital Management — managing $1.5 billion with 40 staff and up 10% in the first five months of this year — has announced it will close, with founder James Chen and most of the team joining US multi-strategy giant ExodusPoint en bloc. Why would a profitable fund choose to shut itself down? The answer lies in a thread of industry logic that began when Segantii collapsed two years ago. 1. Why Was Ovata Swallowed Up? Wasn't It Making Money?

Yes, it was. Ovata returned 10% in the first five months of this year and turned in a solid 2025 as well. $1.5 billion in AUM is not small for an Asia equity strategy. Founder James Chen brings 25 years of experience: a 1990 graduate of SUNY Stony Brook, he worked at Credit Suisse, Bear Stearns, J.P. Morgan and Barclays Capital, and headed BlueCrest Capital's Hong Kong office before founding Ovata in 2017. The Canada Pension Plan Investment Board (CPPIB) was an early backer.

But making money and staying alive are two different things. The fatal flaw of an independent boutique is not performance — it's structure: high fundraising costs, heavy mid- and back-office burdens, and a low size ceiling. Ovata's $1.5 billion was already near the ceiling for a single-strategy boutique in Asia. Going beyond that, without platform-level infrastructure and capital-raising capability, marginal costs would eat into returns.

2. Why ExodusPoint?

ExodusPoint's Asia expansion has been methodical. It returned 18% in 2025 and just completed a $2 billion new raise in June this year, with the Abu Dhabi Investment Council (ADIC) participating. With 8 offices globally, roughly 100 PM teams, and headcount up from 615 in 2022 to 707, it maintains three Asian bases — Hong Kong, Singapore and Tokyo — with Steve Xu overseeing Asia macro trading.

But Asia equities had long been one of its weaker areas. The addition of the Ovata team directly fills that gap. James Chen will become head of Asia equities, while former Ovata head of trading Kiu Ng will serve as co-head of trading for Asia-Pacific — sharing the role with Aaron Davis, hired from Balyasny. Both report to Joe Marcogliese, global head of trading operations.

There's a telling detail in Kiu Ng's background: he spent 5 years as head of trading at Segantii, joined Ovata after Segantii's 2024 closure, and now moves from Ovata to ExodusPoint. The same person, two employers in two years, both times because the previous firm shut down. It's almost a microcosm of the fate of Asian boutique funds.

3. Segantii → Ovata → ExodusPoint: What Does This Chain Tell Us?

After Segantii closed in 2024, the Asian trading talent market saw a fierce round of competition. Ovata absorbed some of Segantii's people (Kiu Ng among them). Now Ovata has closed too, and talent is again flowing toward larger platforms.

This is no coincidence — it's the structural result of platform pressure. Multi-strategy platforms like Millennium, Citadel and ExodusPoint have absorbed the vast majority of global inflows over the past three years. They offer PMs bigger capital pools, stronger mid- and back-office support, ready-made LP relationships and capital-raising capability. The room for independent boutiques keeps shrinking.

For Asia equity strategists, the most rational career path may no longer be starting their own fund, but joining a global multi-strategy platform. Among the Asian PMs I talk to, a growing number treat getting into Millennium/ExodusPoint/Citadel as their career endgame.

4. What Does This Mean for Recruiters and Job Seekers?

Two directions. First, helping platforms find local Asian teams — after absorbing Ovata, ExodusPoint's Asia equities business will scale quickly and needs more PMs, analysts and traders. Second, helping independent strategists find a doorway into platforms — boutiques like Ovata may increasingly opt for acquisition rather than going it alone in the years ahead.

For job seekers: people with global platform experience plus local Asia strategy capability are the most valuable in today's market. James Chen's path — Barclays to BlueCrest to founding Ovata to joining ExodusPoint — is itself a case study in the platform-ization of Asia equity talent.
