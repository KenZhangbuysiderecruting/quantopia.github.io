SIG Triples Its Hong Kong Office: 70 Staff, 20 Open Roles — Who Does ETF Market-Making Hire?

2026-09-23 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

Last week, a US quantitative market maker leased three floors in Central. That alone isn't news. What's striking is that the firm currently has only about 70 people in Hong Kong — and is taking over more than 50,000 square feet.

On September 17, CK Hutchison's property arm confirmed that Susquehanna International Group (SIG) has signed for three floors at Cheung Kong Center II, close to 52,000 sq ft. Its previous office at AIA Central was under 20,000. Close to a threefold expansion in one move.

According to Reuters, SIG is simultaneously posting 20-plus full-time and internship roles in Hong Kong: quant developers, engineers, researchers, compliance, and recruiters. A 70-person team opening 20-plus roles at once — if all filled, headcount grows nearly 30%.

Office space triples, headcount grows 30%. That gap tells the story. What SIG wants in Hong Kong is not more traders, but the people who can build a market-making system.

Why Hong Kong, Why Now: A $900B+ Market

Reuters put it plainly: SIG wants to go deeper into China's ETF market as a liquidity provider.

Some context. By end-2025, China's onshore ETF market had topped RMB 6 trillion, overtaking Japan as Asia's largest, after growing 61.7% in a single year. ETF turnover on the Shanghai Stock Exchange ranks first in Asia and third globally.

ETFs run on market makers quoting continuously on both sides, compressing spreads. The bigger the market and the more products, the thicker the market-making layer needs to be. Per a September 19 Securities Daily report, in September alone public funds issued 49 liquidity-provider announcements, 46 of them new designated market makers.

Foreign market makers and domestic brokers are scaling up on China ETFs at the same time. SIG's three floors are the newest, most visible piece of that trend.

70 People, 20 Roles: The Hiring Mix Has Changed

What interests me most is the role profile. SIG's current Hong Kong openings look different from its historical configuration there.

The classic offshore market-maker outpost in Hong Kong was "front-office trading plus back-office compliance" — a small desk covering APAC hours. What SIG is posting now: Trading Systems Developer, Quantitative Developer, Researcher — people who write trading systems and build infrastructure.

One signal: SIG's job posts explicitly describe a "Greenfield opportunity, as we scale across APAC." These are net-new roles laid down as a foundation for regional expansion. The Hong Kong office's role is shifting — from hosting trades to being the APAC engineering foothold for market-making capability.

For a recruiter, that translates into one concrete question: how many people in Hong Kong can write low-latency C++ or C#, understand option pricing and Greeks, and want to work at a market maker? There were never many, and Singapore and Shanghai are competing for the same pool.

Not an Outlier — a Thickening Line

SIG is not the only market maker moving on Hong Kong.

Jane Street last year leased six floors at Central Harbourfront, over 220,000 sq ft, in an 18.3-billion-HK-dollar five-year lease — Central's largest single leasing deal in decades. IMC has expanded its Hong Kong office twice, hiring quant researchers, traders and engineers with an AI and machine learning focus.

Three of the world's top market makers are all treating Hong Kong as the fulcrum of their Asia market-making business, for the same underlying reason: liquidity provision in Chinese assets — ETFs especially — has moved from "optional" to "must play."

Central's office vacancy rate fell from 14.5% at the start of the year to 9.7% by July, with hedge funds and quantitative trading firms among the main pre-lessees. They are securing floors faster than they can hire the people to fill them. That is the part worth watching.

People Are Harder to Find Than Floors

Offices can be leased ahead of need and systems built ahead of need. People get hired on their own cycle.

The roles SIG wants in Hong Kong span quant development, engineering and research, with a shared requirement: know the market, know the technology, and be production-ready. Supply of this profile in APAC has always been tight, because market makers run deeply proprietary stacks — few people arrive ready-made; most move between peers, or migrate slowly out of investment-bank quant teams.

Which is why SIG's three floors are not really about 52,000 square feet. They are about the dozens of seats that now need filling. The floors are certain. The people are the variable.

Is your firm expanding or contracting in Hong Kong, Singapore or Shanghai this year? I'd genuinely like to know which way the temperature is reading.
