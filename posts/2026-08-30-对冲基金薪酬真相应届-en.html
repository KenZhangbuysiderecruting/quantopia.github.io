Hedge Fund Pay Truths: $227K for New Grads, $2.2M Average for MDs — But a $30M Sleeve Won't Get You $500K

2026-08-30 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

"Aren't hedge fund professionals all on eight-figure salaries?" That myth is as stubborn in Chinese practitioner circles as VIX mean-reversion. A recent eFinancialCareers compensation report based on a sample of 3,000 respondents punctures the illusion rather thoroughly. I am writing this in Q&A form because a statement-style piece would take the long way around. Below are the six most-asked questions, all answered with survey data.

What Do New Grads Actually Get in Year One?

Not RMB 1 million, not RMB 1.5 million. The median total compensation at Analyst and Associate levels is $227K (roughly RMB 1.6 million). That is a median that includes base salary, bonus and sign-on bonus, averaged across New York, London, Singapore and Hong Kong.

Just focus on the word "median" — the top 10% earn over $500K and the top 5% over $1 million, which is what pulls the average up to around $300K. But to earn that reliably, you still have to clear three gates: the resume screen, the interview loop, and the odds.

What About VP and Director Level?

Roughly 3x junior roles, which works out to a $600K–$800K total compensation range. But note — that 3x is not linear growth.

The reason is that the base/bonus mix shifts. At Analyst level, base might be 60% of the package; by VP and Director, base might be only 30%, with everything else coming from bonus. That introduces a concept unfamiliar to many — the deferred bonus.

Hedge fund total comp by level: Analyst and Associate ≈ $227K, with base around 60%. VP and Director ≈ $600K–$800K, with base around 30%. MD ≈ $2.2M, with base around 15% and most of the remainder deferred or subject to clawback.

Do MDs Really Earn $2.2 Million?

The average at MD level is $2.2 million. But it needs unpacking.

First, the base salary itself rarely exceeds $300K. The remaining $1.9M depends entirely on bonus.

Second, a large share of the bonus is deferred — part of what you are "paid" this year only actually vests 12–24 months later, on the condition that you are still there.

Which means that $2.2M average holds only under two conditions: you stay, and performance holds. If performance drops, or you decide to move mid-cycle, you do not get the full bonus.

Why Are Headlines Always About $100 Million Hires?

The Stephen Schurr case went viral — he joined Millennium from Balyasny on a $100 million package. In two years at Balyasny, he had generated $400 million of profit for the fund.

PM bonuses are typically 12%–25% of profit. A top PM's package can reach $50 million a year. But Schurr is a 0.01% outlier.

The most widely quoted line in the industry comes from former Balyasny analyst Jared Kubin — "The industry is full of 25-to-35-year-olds managing $30 million sleeves." The $30 million sleeve is a critical concept.

The $30M sleeve trap, worked through: a $30M sleeve × 10% return = $3M of profit. At a 12–25% cut, the PM's share is $360K–$750K. After tax and with part deferred, take-home is $300K–$500K.

It sounds like a complete PM. But on a $30M sleeve, even at a 10% return, after the standard cut, taxes and deferrals, you would most likely take home less than $500K in practice. That is why the motivational line says "you need a $100M sleeve to break through" — because the $30M sleeve is really a "trap tier": it looks good on paper but does not pay in reality.

What Do Quant Researchers and Technologists Really Earn?

eFinancialCareers scored every function separately. Top-tier quant researchers have a median total comp of $800K, top technologists $700K, and risk professionals $500K.

By comparison, senior analysts at some top quant firms on a "good pod" pull in $1–3 million in a normal year. That figure is not driven by sleeve size; it is driven by the pod's overall return.

Which is why, when a quant researcher from China moves to an overseas platform, the explosion in earnings rarely comes from base — it comes from the pod's overall PnL. Two researchers with near-identical resumes can land in different pods and see bonuses diverge by 3–5x, routinely.

What About ML Engineers? AI Is Hot — So Do They Earn More?

This is a counter-intuitive counterexample. Kadoa's August data shows 37 hedge funds hiring for 154 ML engineer roles. The biggest hirers include Point72 (14), BlackRock (12), Optiver (12), WorldQuant (10), Jump (9) and Tower (9).

But the median disclosed salary is only $214K — half of what top-tier quant researchers earn.

The reason is not complicated: ML engineer roles are plentiful and have a lower bar than researchers (no PhD required, no alpha experience required), so the market naturally flattens the price. The truly high-paying roles at the intersection of AI and hedge funds are "researchers who can turn ML models into alpha" — not engineers who write TensorFlow. These are two different things.
