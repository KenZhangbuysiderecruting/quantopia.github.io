AMAC Names 9 Unreachable Private Fund Managers — 858 Deregistered This Year

2026-09-13 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

On September 4, AMAC (the Asset Management Association of China) published a notice naming nine private fund managers it could not reach. Some of them still have products running.

This is not a simple missing-contact notice. It is the first public regulatory action since the new disclosure rules took effect on September 1.

First, what "unreachable" actually means

In AMAC's language, "unreachable" is a technical term, not a character judgement.

According to the notice, the association could not make contact by phone, email or SMS. The process has three steps:

  • Within five working days of the notice, the firm must submit a status report on its own initiative.
  • Failing that, it is designated unreachable and publicly listed.
  • If nothing is submitted after one month of public listing, its private fund manager registration is cancelled.

From notice to cancellation is a little over a month. These nine firms are currently inside the first five-working-day window.

One easily missed detail: being named does not mean something went wrong. A firm may simply have moved office, changed its contact person, or forgotten to update its details in the association's system. Those five working days exist precisely for that.

Who the nine are

Per the notice, the nine managers are:

  • Jiangxi Fule Investment (P1064240)
  • Shanghai Yinshi Private Fund (P1015085)
  • Beijing Jinshi Zhiyuan Investment (P1025134)
  • Beijing Xiangyuan Private Fund (P1060701)
  • Shanghai Jiushi Private Fund (P1073713)
  • Shenzhen Lituo Ruihe (P1067195)
  • Hebei Qinwei Investment (P1061406)
  • Beijing Jinshi Zhiyuan Asset (P1004023)
  • Hangzhou Xundong Asset (P1061468)

According to verification by The Paper, some still have live products — Shanghai Yinshi's "Yinshi No. 10" among them, along with several funds under Beijing Jinshi Zhiyuan.

The firm cannot be reached, the products are still running. That is exactly why the regulator wants a status report within five working days.

Why now

Timing matters. On September 1, the Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Information Disclosure of Private Investment Funds took effect. One clause requires custodians to report promptly to AMAC when they find a manager unreachable.

That single line changes the detection mechanism. Previously it relied on periodic AMAC reviews and investor complaints. Now the custodian is the first sensor — the party handling money and valuations every day is the first to know contact has been lost.

The notice was published September 4 and publicised September 7. From the new rules taking effect to the first public naming, three working days passed.

The same day, the CSRC opened public consultation on the Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Private Investment Fund Fundraising.

Putting 858 cancellations in scale

According to The Paper, counting AMAC's own website data, 858 private fund managers had been deregistered year-to-date as of September 8, 2026.

The number needs scale to mean anything. AMAC-registered private fund managers number in the tens of thousands; 858 is roughly several percent. The proportion is modest, but structurally it concentrates in smaller firms — the smaller the firm, the harder it is to spread a fixed compliance cost.

In April and July, AMAC had already cancelled 21 unreachable private funds across two batches. The pace has visibly picked up since the second quarter.

What it means for people in the industry

In recruiting, I have seen plenty of candidates treat "having a private fund licence" as proof that a firm is stable. That yardstick needs updating.

A licence is not for life. It is closer to a permit that has to keep meeting conditions: information updated, products reported, custodians engaged. Break any link and a firm can be named.

When evaluating a platform, a few things are worth checking in advance: whether the AMAC registration is current, how many products are under management, whether there is a stable custodian relationship, and whether the compliance officer is in post. All of this is on the AMAC website — ten minutes is enough.

For people at smaller firms there is another reality: compliance cost is fixed, and the smaller the firm, the harder to absorb. That is a cost-structure problem, not a capability problem.

A question worth asking: when you choose a platform, do you check the AMAC registration, or mainly look at AUM and performance?

One line to close

Nine firms named, a five-working-day window, 858 cancellations year-to-date. Together they describe a mechanism that is now running.

The new rules put the duty to detect unreachability on the custodian, effectively installing a sensor inside the money trail. How well it works will show up in the notice frequency over the next few months.
