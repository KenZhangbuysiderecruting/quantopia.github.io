China's New Private Fund Rule Quietly Rewrites Who Can Buy Private Equity Products

2026-09-07 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

"Inside shares" pitched on WeChat Moments. "Principal-protected, high-yield" slogans in the salesperson's mouth. Money wired to a corporate or even personal account. Those three old recipes in China's private fund sales have just been named in a single draft regulation — one by one.

The Document, in Numbers

On September 4, the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) released a draft of the "Measures for the Supervision of Private Investment Fund Raising (For Public Comments)". The feedback window closes on October 4. Seven chapters, 45 articles. The scope runs end to end: who can buy, through whom, into which account, and how the paperwork must be kept.

The industry's first reaction was unanimous: the qualified investor definition has been rewritten.

Three Tests, Not One

In the past, the test for buying a private fund was simple: do you have the money? The new rule splits the criteria into three tests, all of which must be passed:

  • At least two years of investment experience in securities, funds, futures, or equity
  • Family financial assets of at least RMB 5 million, or family net financial assets of at least RMB 3 million, or personal annual income of at least RMB 500,000 averaged over the past three years
  • At least RMB 1 million per fund invested, with the first tranche actually paid in no lower than RMB 1 million

Note the shift: from "personal assets" to "family financial assets". Wang Tieniu, director of the Jixian Jixin fund evaluation center, read this as a closing of an old loophole — "judging only by how much money you have, not whether you know how to invest".

The Harder Bar for Derivatives, Single Names, and Overseas

Article 7 hits harder. For private funds investing in a single underlying, overseas assets, or over-the-counter derivatives, the natural-person threshold doubles: four years of investment experience, family financial assets of at least RMB 10 million, and family net financial assets of at least RMB 6 million.

Translation: to buy a product with a derivative structure, money alone isn't enough. You also need to prove you "understand".

Splitting, Pooling, and the 200-Person Cap

A passage in the document deserves to be read word by word. No organization or individual may split or transfer private fund shares or收益权, or set up multiple private funds for a single underlying, to lower the qualified investor threshold or break through the investor-count limit.

A single fund may not have more than 200 investors in total. When capital is pooled via asset-management products, partnerships, or similar vehicles, look-through is required, and the underlying investors are counted together.

The path of a few friends pooling RMB 1 million through a channel is, in theory, welded shut.

Narrower Channels, Tighter Money Trails

Raising channels narrow at the same time. Private funds can only be raised by the manager itself or by licensed sales agencies appointed by it. They cannot be marketed to unspecified objects via internet platforms. Self-operated platforms can only address investors who have passed suitability review. Principal-protected or return-guaranteed promises, inducement in advertising, and false advertising are explicitly listed as prohibited.

Money safety now carries double insurance: a dedicated settlement account supervised by a commercial bank or brokerage, with the account property segregated from the manager's own assets. The eight-character principle — "sellers responsible, buyers self-accountable" — has been operationalized into an executable workflow.

Where the Pain Lands

The cost of non-compliance goes up. Failure to follow the contract, account-opening, and record-keeping requirements brings a warning or public reprimand plus a fine of up to RMB 100,000. Offenses involving financial safety with harmful consequences carry a fine of up to RMB 200,000. In serious cases, the regulator can impose a market-entry ban, and suspected criminal conduct is referred to the judiciary. The fine amount itself is not the point — "market-entry ban plus criminal referral" is the sword hanging overhead.

Data from the Asset Management Association of China (AMAC) shows that as of the end of July, the country's private fund AUM stood at RMB 25.73 trillion. This is the first administrative measure landed under the "1+N+X" rule framework that followed the State Council's June policy document — and it targets only the raising stage.

Xing Shi Investment's read: the raising layer is being truly "de-retailed", returning to a "private" placement model aimed at high-net-worth individuals. The head of marketing at one hundred-billion-RMB private fund was more blunt — short-term fundraising will get harder, but for managers who are genuinely strong, the higher investor quality is a positive.

Flip it around to see who hurts most: small shops that depend on unlicensed channels and WeChat-Moments connections to move product, gray-area salespeople who help clients "pool-and-hold", and managers with large natural-person books in derivatives or overseas structured products. Their client mix is being redrawn.

What This Means for Quant

For quant private funds especially, this hits. Product lines tied to OTC derivatives now face a double-high bar on natural-person LPs: RMB 10 million in family assets plus four years of investment history. The natural-person book can only shift toward institutions and high-net-worth professional investors. This read is derived from the draft's clauses — it is not a regulator-named category.

As regulatory granularity gets finer, the value of compliance, suitability management, and investor education roles is moving up. In the past, "having the role" was enough. Going forward, the question is whether the role can withstand look-through inspection. This round of shake-out does not wash out small shops — it washes out the compliance-weak.

Compliance cost in private fund is becoming the entry ticket for the industry.
