Ubiquant Adds 36 People: China's 147 RMB100bn Funds Grew Headcount by 136

2026-11-18 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

In the half-year when assets shrank, headcount grew. Both numbers sit in the same dataset.

Chinese quant manages about RMB 3 trillion. More than 70% of the quant long-only funds launched this year are under water, and the largest managers shed more than RMB 10 billion of assets in the second half.

Over the same six months, inside the 7,801 staff employed by 147 managers above RMB 100 billion, the 17 firms with more than 100 people went from 2,248 to 2,384. A net gain of 136. Exactly one firm shrank, by one person.

How the 136 was counted

The accounting first, because this is the part most easily misread.

2,248 and 2,384 are totals for two separate dates, and the roster changed in between. Pingfanghe went from 93 to 104 and Blackwing from 91 to 100; both crossed the 100-person line only in this half-year. So the 136 contains a membership effect. It cannot be written as "these 17 firms hired 136 people."

There are also two versions of the headcount data. Youyou Wealth lists Waimao Trust at 268, Ubiquant at 203, Lingjun at 172, Shanghai Kuande at 156 and Gaoyi at 152; Simuwang's own article lists Waimao Trust at 267, Ubiquant at 188, Lingjun at 173 and Kuande at 157. The gap of a dozen or so reflects registration timing and scope. The two cannot be mixed in one article; this piece uses the first throughout, as of end-October.

Where the 136 landed: five firms took 74%

The gains cluster. Ubiquant added 36 to reach 203, Tianyan 25, Kuande 18, Yinno 11, Pingfanghe 11 to reach 104. Together that is 101 people, or 74% of the 136.

The other 12 firms split 35 people, fewer than three each on average.

Both newcomers are new to the 100-person line, not to the RMB100bn club. The four managers that crossed RMB100bn for the first time this year — Tianlu, Yushu, Shanghai Qingyan and Leshui — appear nowhere on this list.

Which roles

The two firms that just crossed 100 people lead their 2027 campus recruiting with machine learning and data science roles. Another RMB100bn quant lists quantitative strategy researchers alongside machine learning researchers, with R&D at roughly 70% of staff and master's or PhD degrees at about 85%.

The product side matches. In the first ten months, 12,673 private securities funds were filed, up 27.2% year on year. The 125 managers above RMB100bn filed 4,404 of them, 34.75% of the total, an average of 35.23 each against 4.76 for the industry — more than seven times.

Every extra product means another NAV disclosure, another unit registry, another valuation check, another compliance record. Those seats produce no alpha, but they decide whether a product can run legally at all. The denser the issuance, the more rigid this kind of headcount becomes.

One number pointing the other way

If AI really is lifting productivity, headcount should be falling.

Xu Shunan, founder of Yinno Asset, offered a concrete version in Shanghai Securities News on 12 November: a portfolio manager used to need a research team of a dozen people for factor mining, portfolio construction and coding; going forward, one PM with one or two people and a large model could match what twenty do today.

Three days earlier, Apollo co-president Scott Kleinman told Business Insider the firm would grow headcount 3% to 5% a year instead of its usual 12% to 15%, with AI the primary driver. On a base of 6,140 staff at the end of 2025, that is 430 to 737 jobs a year that will not be added.

The same week, Apollo's chief economist Torsten Sløk published a report saying AI has delivered no financial payoff outside the technology sector. One firm, two statements, both on the record.

Headcount is rising; what is changing is the mix

Both explanations hold. It depends which layer is growing.

Higher output per person shrinks the execution layer. That is the first layer. The layer that raises output per person — data platforms, training infrastructure, engineered factor factories, backtesting and risk systems — needs more people, and more expensive ones. The leaders are adding the second layer and subtracting from the first.

Which is why I read the 136 as a structural signal rather than a volume signal: the part of an organisation that can be expressed as a headcount is contracting, and the part that cannot is getting heavier. At the total level nothing looks different. Underneath, the mix has already turned over once.

That is inference, not a filing. Public data gives headcount, not grade or function.

The roster itself is thinning

263 managers have crossed RMB100bn at some point; 147 remain. Only 51 have stayed above the line for three full years without a single month below it, taking an average of 7.1 years to get there. Five — Ningquan, Panjing, Shanghai Lingren, Shenzhi and Yanfu — made it in under three years from founding.

Of the 147, pure quant managers number 75, or 51%; discretionary 57, hybrid 15.

So two-thirds of this list is not permanent. Where the 136 people landed, the data does not say which firm got what. That the gains concentrated among a few long-standing names is clear enough.

For anyone looking at offers

Three kinds of seat, three different negotiations.

Platform and engineering. Data pipelines, training and backtest infrastructure, trading systems. Portable skills, recognised across firms and industries; the conversation is about system scale and complexity.

Research. Factors, portfolios, ML models. The conversation is about a verifiable research record and live P&L attribution; the leaders now ask how large a book you ran on your own.

Middle and back office. Valuation, disclosure, compliance, operations. The conversation is about process experience and regulatory interfaces. The denser the issuance, the scarcer the people — but the ceiling sits inside a function line.

Three questions cover an interview: which layer did this team add to this year? Whose scorecard does my output feed? Is next year's headcount going to research or to platform?

Boundaries

Headcount is not headcount quality. The 203 at Ubiquant include research, engineering, middle office and marketing, and public data does not split them. Waimao Trust's 268 belongs to a trust company and cannot be benchmarked against a pure quant manager. Registration counts at the Asset Management Association and month-end payroll counts differ, and the two data vendors differ by a dozen people. Trend beats absolute level.
