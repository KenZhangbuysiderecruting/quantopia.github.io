D.E. Shaw at $125bn: The Best Strategies Are Now Employees-Only

2026-11-05 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

Clients get a new 0.12% fee line and lockups stretched to four years. In the same week, the firm announces that starting January, some of its best investment ideas will be reserved for employees — clients can't buy in. This is not a struggling shop fighting for survival. It's D.E. Shaw, headquartered in New York, which just crossed $125 billion in assets.

Roughly $90bn sits in hedge fund strategies, $30bn in long-oriented investments, $5bn in privates.

0.12% is loose change — and also a signal

Bloomberg landed a rare interview with the decision-makers: a seven-person Executive Committee that governs collectively — the "hive-mind CEO," as outsiders call it. The reason for the new charge was stated plainly: AI infrastructure, compute, data, and the talent war.

0.12% sounds like pocket change. Multiply it by roughly $90bn of hedge fund strategies and you get well over $100 million a year — enough to fund a sizable research and compute team several times over.

Hedge funds aren't public companies. Their cost structures aren't disclosed, and fee schedules are the only window outsiders get. Putting "technology" on the invoice as its own line item says something blunt: the cost of the AI arms race has grown large enough to bill clients for.

Four years locked up — against what?

The official rationale: room for long-horizon, complex strategies, without redemptions breaking the rhythm. But in the same interview, executives volunteered a risk detail: the firm has avoided highly leveraged Treasury basis trades, citing the danger that forced liquidations in extreme volatility would amplify shocks.

A firm whose AUM grew $27bn in a year is voluntarily locking up client liquidity. Read together, the two moves say more than either does alone.

The line worth watching: alpha kept in-house

The employees-only fund launches in January 2027, with half its initial capital coming from the flagship Composite. Clients get no direct access.

Renaissance did this more than a decade ago. So did Two Sigma. D.E. Shaw was the last of the big quants to follow — and now it has. Staff and partner money across D.E. strategies already exceeds $15bn.

Some clients are unhappy. They already wanted to invest more, and now find the best ideas kept inside the door. Executive committee member Edwin Jager, quoted directly: "It's clear that prop shops are increasingly competing for similar types of alpha."

Translated: the best alpha is being reclassified from "client asset" to "employee benefit."

Why now

The interview names three rivals: Citadel, Millennium, Jane Street. Multi-manager platforms compete for the same people; proprietary trading firms compete for the same kind of alpha. D.E. Shaw has long styled itself as the firm with the less cutthroat culture — and now it, too, has launched an employee fund. When rivals bid high enough, culture gets repriced too.

A note on how this firm decides things. In 1998, Max Stone — now 57, then not yet 30 — nearly blew up the firm with a drawdown of roughly 30%. "If I had been fired, no part of me would have thought that was unfair," he said later. Twenty-eight years on, he sits on the seven-member executive committee and chairs the Risk Committee. The firm runs on collective decision-making against star culture, with job one being that nobody blows it up again.

Three practical reads for practitioners

  • When weighing an offer, price "can I invest in our own flagship" as real compensation. $15bn of house money isn't decoration — it's a retention tool and a profit-sharing mechanism.
  • The technology surcharge will likely spread. AI cost pressure isn't unique to one firm; 0.12% is the first explicitly priced line item. Watch who follows, and whether anyone dares charge 0.5%.
  • The wall between quant and discretionary is thinning. The king of quant raised $6bn for a fund run by human PMs, and for the first time pitched it to wealthy individuals — business boundaries have always been soft where the money is.
