Paul Tudor Jones' Quant Unit Is Spinning Off: 21-Year Goldman Veteran to Head Hong Kong

2026-07-27 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

Paul Tudor Jones' quant unit is preparing to "split off". According to a Bloomberg report on July 8, Xantium Group — the quant investment firm managing several billion dollars in assets — is being spun out of Tudor Investment Corp. In mid-May, it registered a subsidiary in Hong Kong. The person tapped to lead the Asia business: Christina Ma, a 21-year Goldman Sachs veteran. This is more than a restructuring at Tudor. It's the latest case of a major US multi-strategy/macro fund "relocating" its quant DNA to Asia — and another signal that competition for quant talent in Hong Kong is heating up. 💡 Go ahead and share this now: Hong Kong is about to gain another top-tier quant platform.

Quantopia · 2026-07-27 · Overseas hedge funds · Asia expansion

Q1 | What Is Xantium?

Xantium Group is still part of Tudor Investment Corp. for now. Its website says it trades across global public and private equity, options, FX and bond markets. Its AUM is in the "several billion dollars" range — with intelligence from With Intelligence suggesting Xantium could debut with more than $5 billion in assets when independent, potentially one of the largest quant fund spin-offs in recent years.

That figure has not been officially confirmed, and Xantium representatives declined to comment on the spin-off. But the Hong Kong Companies Registry filings and Bloomberg's reporting from people familiar with the matter have made the picture clear enough.

Q2 | Why Spin Off from Tudor?

Quant units spinning off from large funds has become increasingly common in recent years.

The core logic: quant strategies' infrastructure, talent incentives and brand positioning increasingly diverge from traditional macro/discretionary strategies. Placing the quant team in an independent entity allows more flexibility in fundraising, hiring and carry allocation — and makes it easier to attract outside investors.

Tudor's typical spin-off model: Tudor retains a stake and economic interest while letting Xantium operate independently. This is essentially the same logic as Citadel Securities' separation from Citadel and Point72's quant-team expansion.

🔄 Mid-article share: from sell side to buy side to running her own show, Christina Ma's path is a textbook route for senior Asian quant executives.

Q3 | Who Is Christina Ma?

Christina Ma's resume is highly representative in Asian quant/trading circles.

  • Goldman Sachs (21 years): led Greater China equities and sales businesses, later served as head of One Delta trading for Asia (ex-Japan)
  • HSBC (2 years): head of global banking for Asia-Pacific
  • Xantium: to oversee Asia business

One Delta is Goldman's electronic trading/quant execution platform. Ma spent years in this role, meaning deep accumulated knowledge of Asian market liquidity structure, institutional client networks and quant execution strategies — precisely the background Xantium needs most as it builds out its Hong Kong office.

Q4 | Why Hong Kong?

Xantium is hardly the only US quant firm choosing Hong Kong.

The recent trend is unmistakable: Millennium-seeded new quant funds like Third Epsilon and Hippocampus base their Asia headquarters in Hong Kong; Citadel Securities is expanding across Asia with nearly half of new hires based in Hong Kong; Point72 moved into The Henderson in Central; and Jane Street and QRT are also significantly expanding their Hong Kong office space.

Hong Kong's advantages haven't changed: time-zone coverage of Asian markets, low taxes, a relatively deep talent pool, and a gateway to mainland China and Southeast Asian capital. For Xantium, the Hong Kong subsidiary is a bridgehead for Asia expansion, not the final destination.

Q5 | What Does It Mean for Asian Quant Talent?

Xantium's independence plus a Hong Kong office means Asian quant roles gain another "buy-side benchmark".

It doesn't need researchers with purely local backgrounds — it needs people with global perspective, cross-asset quant expertise and the ability to build a team from scratch on an independent platform. For those doing index enhancement at Chinese quant firms, this could be a career upgrade; for overseas PMs/quants, it's a window to return to Asia as an "early employee".

But the window won't stay open long. Opportunities like this usually concentrate in the 6–12 months after a spin-off — that's when team structure, strategy direction and headcount get locked in.
