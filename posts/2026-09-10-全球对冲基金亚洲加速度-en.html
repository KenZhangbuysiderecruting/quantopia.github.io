From 56 to 100: How Global Hedge Funds Are Accelerating Into Asia

2026-09-10 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

In the same week, four of the world's top hedge funds made their move in Asia at the same time.

ExodusPoint is growing its Asia headcount from 56 to about 100 — an 80% increase. Point72 plans to double both its Japan team and capital allocation, targeting 100 people. Jump Trading is adding 11,000 square feet in Hong Kong and hiring quant and AI talent. Citadel, meanwhile, announced a change of leadership on its Asia equities trading desk.

All four point to the same thing: Asia is moving from an optional allocation to a market the biggest firms have to be in. It is not "the next America" — it has its own rhythm, and its own talent scarcity.

ExodusPoint: 56 to 100, Building Infrastructure Across Three Cities

Bloomberg reported on September 8 that multi-strategy platform ExodusPoint Capital Management plans to grow its Asia headcount from 56 by nearly 80% to about 100 this year, across Hong Kong, Singapore and Tokyo. The firm has reserved over 20,000 square feet of office space across the three cities, enough for 180 people. Last month it signed a deal to double its Hong Kong office to 11,000 square feet, with the local team expected to reach 50 by year-end.

ExodusPoint was founded in 2018 by Michael Gelband, formerly Millennium's global head of fixed income, with about $8 billion in launch capital — one of the largest hedge fund launches in history. Six years on, the Asia team was only 56 people. Now it is scaling up in earnest. This is not a hit-and-run; it is a five-year bet on Asia as a primary battlefield.

Point72: Doubling Down on Japan, Betting on Corporate Governance Reform

Bloomberg reported on September 7 that Steve Cohen's Point72 plans to double both its Japan headcount and capital allocation over the next few years, targeting 100 people. Japan head Shinji Ogawa (ex-JPMorgan, appointed November 2025) said in an interview that corporate governance reform may push more Japanese companies to re-examine capital allocation and redirect funds toward growth investments.

Ogawa disclosed that Point72 is seeking talent across fundamental research, macro and quantitative analysis, and has already added internal trainers and a dedicated campus recruiter to its Tokyo office. Point72 established its Japan office in 2011; its AUM stands at about $58.5 billion as of July.

The broader context: Brevan Howard plans to open a Japan office this year, and Marshall Wace is targeting a 2027 launch. Add in ExodusPoint and Fortress, which already have Tokyo presences — Japan is shifting from an "optional market" to a consensus bet across global hedge funds.

Jump Trading: Hong Kong ECM Rebounds, Quant and AI Hiring in Parallel

Bloomberg and HedgeWeek reported on September 5 that Chicago-based Jump Trading is adding 11,000 square feet (about 1,022 square metres) at One IFC in Hong Kong, expanding to a new floor while also enlarging its existing space. Sources said the firm plans to hire for quant and AI roles in the city.

The backdrop is a revival in Hong Kong market activity. Hong Kong Exchange data shows listed options volume and open interest have hit record highs for three consecutive years, with open interest as of September 4 already exceeding one-third of the full-year 2023 total. Jump has also surfaced in Hong Kong ECM activity — when Zhongsheng Technology listed in Hong Kong, Jump was a disclosed cornerstone investor; the stock rose 50% on day one.

It is not just Jump. Jane Street leased six floors at Central Yards last year, QRT expanded at Two IFC, Point72 enlarged at Henderson Tower, and SIG is growing too. Global quant firms are collectively returning to Hong Kong — not because rent is cheaper, but because liquidity in Chinese equities, ETFs and derivatives markets is back.

Citadel: A Change of Guard on the Asia Desk

Also this week. Bloomberg reported on September 4 that Citadel's Asia trading head AJ Bolduc had left, about four months after she joined the roughly $77 billion AUM firm in Hong Kong in May. Bolduc was previously Goldman Sachs's APAC Cash Trading head, promoted to MD in November 2025. Ed Phillips (ex-UBS London, joined Citadel in March 2025) takes over; the rest of the team remains unchanged.

Bolduc took on the ECM investment business on arrival — not just secondary-market execution, but also IPOs and private placements. The team was expanding throughout: Luis Felipe Avi (ex-GS), Alex Wood (ex-MS) and Renee Sun (ex-Citi) all joined recently. It is a desk growing fast and still finding its shape, and matching people to newly created seats simply takes time.

Citadel has committed to significantly increasing the International Equities division's capital allocation to Asia over the next two to three years. That division is led by Drew Gillanders, based in London. The direction has not changed.

Money Is Moving to Asia — the People Cycle Has to Keep Up

One more data point from the same week: Millennium Management's AUM has reached $97 billion (Bloomberg, September 8), with $22 billion in new capital commitments to be completed in October. AQR has surpassed $140 billion, DE Shaw is around $90 billion — six firms now exceed $75 billion in AUM, a historical first.

Money is accelerating toward the top institutions, and those institutions are accelerating toward Asia. The turnover cycle for senior Asia seats is now moving in step with global markets — which is itself a sign that Asia is no longer a peripheral allocation.

If you are looking at Asia, what matters more to you: how committed a platform is to building locally, or how long this round of capital allocation lasts?
