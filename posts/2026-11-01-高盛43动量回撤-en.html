Goldman's 43%: The S&P Fell 0.93% and the Momentum Book Gave Back the Year

1 Nov 2026 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

In October the S&P 500 lost 0.93%. Goldman's flagship momentum book fell 43% from its June peak and gave back every gain it had made this year.

Same month. Same market. The index barely moved; the book went back to where it started.

Tony Pasquariello, who heads Goldman's hedge fund coverage, put the two numbers side by side: over the past four months the S&P 500's cumulative absolute move was about 4 percentage points, while the GS High Beta Momentum basket (GSPRHIMO) moved about 90. He was restrained about it, saying only that volatility below the surface would persist.

Metric October
S&P 500-0.93%
Nasdaq 100-3.3% (over $1.2trn of constituent market cap erased)
Goldman high-beta momentum basket-43% from June peak, year's gains wiped out
S&P 500 equal weight+4.8% (9 of 11 sectors up)

Sources: Goldman Sachs strategy research, Bloomberg market data, as of 30 Oct 2026

Rows three and four are the whole point: in the same October, equal-weight money made money and momentum money lost it.

The pain was concentrated. On 29 October Alphabet fell 12.6% in a day after reporting negative free cash flow of $9.1bn and capex of $51.4bn for the quarter. The next day Intel fell 28.6%, its worst single session since Bloomberg records began in 1982, wiping out roughly $140bn of market value. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.9% on 30 October, its worst day since June.

But on that same 29 October, 406 S&P 500 constituents rose. Alphabet's slide dragged the index down 0.3% while more than 400 stocks advanced; Bloomberg data back to 1996 shows 987 prior sessions with 400-plus advancers, and the index closed higher every single time.

That is the most counter-intuitive layer of this October: it wasn't stocks falling, it was weights falling.

The second layer is positioning. Goldman prime brokerage data shows hedge funds dumping tech at the fastest two-week pace in over a decade, with US net leverage down to a five-year low. Note the framing: a five-year low, not an ordinary pullback. In June 2025 gross hedge fund leverage tracked by Goldman was still at 294%, a five-year high. A little over a year later, directional exposure has been cut to the floor.

The third layer is Goldman arguing with itself. In the same week, two widely followed strategists reached opposite conclusions. Pasquariello wrote that S&P 500 earnings are excellent but growth is 'increasingly clearly peaking', the primary bull trend intact with fading upside momentum, and that he is not making large bets here. Mark Wilson in London said this week's hyperscaler results answered the AI return-on-investment question 'fairly definitively', staying bullish on the back of $1.1trn of combined backlog at Google Cloud and AWS.

Same firm, same week, two directions. The split is itself the information: there is no consensus to follow this time.

The fourth layer is the easiest to miss. Retail and institutions are moving opposite ways. In the same week institutions cut net leverage to a five-year low, triple-levered semiconductor ETFs took in $6.1bn, a record, and then lost 20.5% the next session.

One layer deeper, the problem isn't valuation, it's cash flow. Alphabet's quarterly free cash flow was -$9.1bn; Amazon's was -$33.5bn on $77.3bn of capex; Microsoft grew Azure 48% but commercial bookings fell 12% and it issued $25bn of debt. Goldman's credit strategists say AI default risk has tripled since January, and CoreWeave's unsecured bonds now trade beyond 1,000bp.

The giants have started borrowing to burn compute. That is what genuinely separates this from the 2024 drawdowns: those killed valuations, this one kills the question of who pays.

Macro didn't help either. Brent settled at $117.64 on Friday, the 10-year Treasury yield broke below 4.9% to a six-week low, core PCE is at 3.1%, and the market has priced a December hike as a done deal.

I'll leave myself room here: the -0.93% is calm manufactured by an average. The real signal is the 43% — it says the most profitable style of the past two years failed all at once this October. How badly, the next two or three months of leverage data will tell. Whether a five-year low is a floor or a way station, it is too early to call.
