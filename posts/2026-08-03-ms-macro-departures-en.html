Morgan Stanley's Asia Macro Team Lost Nearly 40 People: The Singapore Trading Talent Exodus

2026-08-03 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

In January 2026, a rates trader at Morgan Stanley Singapore was just promoted to Managing Director. Five months later, he left. On its own, that's nothing — he's just one of nearly 40 departures from Morgan Stanley's Asia macro team. According to eFinancialCareers, one Morgan Stanley insider used a single word: unprecedented. Another called it an exodus. Where did these 40 people go? Why did they leave? Compiled from public international media reporting, this is the first time the Chinese-language world has systematically covered the story. 1. Leaving Five Months After an MD Promotion: Almost an Insult on the Street

Let's start with the most representative case. This trader joined Morgan Stanley Singapore in 2020, having previously traded rates at Citi and Goldman Sachs. On January 8, 2026, he appeared on Morgan Stanley's official list of 184 newly promoted MDs — one of the highest ranks a bank trader can reach.

Five months later, he left. His precise destination has not been fully confirmed by international media.

Leaving five months after an MD promotion is almost an insult on the Street. But for a rates trader, Singapore's buy-side market in 2026 is simply on fire — Balyasny, Dymon Capital Asia and Jain Global are all scrambling for macro PMs, and local recruiters say hiring is going "full throttle".

2. Nearly 40 Departures: Who They Are, Where They Went

According to three tracking reports from eFinancialCareers dated June 12, June 16 and July 15, 2026, the departure list spans MD, ED, VP and SVP levels across Morgan Stanley's Hong Kong, Singapore and Tokyo bases, covering roughly 18 months from early 2025 to July 2026.

For the full list of names, please refer to the original eFinancialCareers articles (a paid subscription site). This piece does not recount each individual; it focuses only on industry-level analysis of where people went.

A few rank-level signals worth noting:

1. MD level: at least one rates MD left five months after promotion

2. ED level: includes a senior ED with more than 16 years at MS who also chose to leave

3. Head level: the co-head of Asia sales, the head of Asia & Australia non-linear rates trading, the head of Asia fixed income macro distribution, and the head of Asia credit analysis — among other key roles — all departed

4. VP and below: a large number of VP-level traders and sales professionals left, mostly for other banks

3. Why Did They Leave? Three Signals

First, the buy side is poaching. Singapore's macro PM market is running full throttle. Balyasny, Dymon Capital Asia and Jain Global are all hiring. For sell-side traders, moving to a hedge fund means higher upside and a decision seat closer to capital.

Second, the team is shrinking. Nearly 40 people leaving one team in 18 months is abnormal by any standard. Morgan Stanley officially declined to comment. But the data speaks for itself — when a team loses nearly 40 people, either its competitiveness has declined or external pull has become too strong.

Third, not everyone who left was fought for. Reports say some departees were bid back with counter-offers — unsuccessfully. That tells you Morgan Stanley's competitiveness in Asia macro, whether on pay or career progression, has been matched or overtaken by the buy side and other banks.

4. What This Means for Recruiters

This departure map is a ready-made talent-mapping framework. Three directions are worth watching:

1. Those who went to hedge funds — Dymon Asia took at least 2, and Balyasny, Modular AM and Qube each hired. They've just landed in new roles, but they'll be high-value targets in the next round of moves.

2. Those who went to other banks — Barclays hired the most, at least 3, with Goldman, Citi, BMO, JPM, DB, BNP and ANZ all taking people. They're moving between banks, which tends to be more stable than jumping to hedge funds.

3. Those who went the entrepreneurial route — someone founded 3B Investment Partners. A small new fund may have hiring needs.

For macro traders still on the sell side, the scale of this exodus sends a clear signal: in 2026, the door into Singapore's buy side is wider than ever. The question is no longer whether to move to a hedge fund, but which one and when.
