Balyasny's $200M Bill: Three Kinds of Talent, One Platform

2026-10-25 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

$200 million. That is what Balyasny has paid out this year to recruit senior investors, per Bloomberg's October 14 report. The number is not a record for a multi-strategy platform — but where it went is worth reading. Within one month, three very different roles landed.

First, the list.

New arrivalRoleFromKey number
Antoine IskandarSenior PMMillennium (Zug, Switzerland)Managed over $600m
Limin YangFormer CIONebula Research & DevelopmentEx-MD, quantitative research, BlackRock alternatives
Halla YangSenior quant researcherCitadel data strategies groupLed the group for 5 years

Put the three hires side by side and the platform is buying a complete stack: signal, strategy, capital. Layer by layer.

The bottom layer: data signal. Halla Yang spent eight years at Citadel, five of them as co-head and then sole head of the data strategies group, using AI to extract investment signals from alternative data such as satellite imagery. He left in April 2025, spent over a year out of the industry, and joined Balyasny's Chicago office this month. The earlier resume: data scientist at McKinsey, quant researcher at Jump Trading, VP at Goldman Sachs. One rarer credential — Kaggle grandmaster, a distinction held by only a few hundred people worldwide.

The middle layer: strategy. Limin Yang was CIO of Nebula Research and Development, running statistical arbitrage, after serving as managing director in BlackRock's alternatives quantitative research team and holding portfolio and prop-trading roles at Laurion Capital and Credit Suisse. Nebula itself has a history: founder Michael Graves, who came from Cubist Systematic Strategies, died in 2022, and seed investor Paloma withdrew its capital afterwards.

The top layer: capital and risk budget. Antoine Iskandar managed more than $600 million at Millennium, running a team in equities, relative value and equity derivatives. Per Bloomberg's sourcing, the team had accumulated profits over the prior three years before losing tens of millions of dollars in 2026; he had also been in preliminary talks with Millennium about spinning out his own fund, and chose Balyasny instead.

Here is the reversal worth pausing on: why would anyone take a PM mid-drawdown? Bloomberg's explanation is blunt — on a new platform, performance fees are calculated from zero, with no need to dig out of the old book first. A loss-making PM is not a depreciated asset. That is the multi-strategy industry pricing its own logic.

Bloomberg reported another move the same day: a Millennium trader plans to spin out a fund and then join Balyasny. Neither Iskandar nor either firm commented.

Inside Balyasny, units like Longaeva Partners already run on the firm's platform infrastructure. Millennium, Citadel and Point72 are likewise spending on senior investors. The talent budget in multi-strategy shows no sign of shrinking — the unit of competition has simply changed: no longer the individual star, but an entire capability layer. A head of data strategies sits across equities, macro and credit teams at once; what gets bought is platform-wide infrastructure.

With the stack assembled, the next question is how much capital the stack can carry.

Next issue: October's third private-fund self-purchase. After Kaiwei's 25 million yuan, the year-to-date total just passed 1.61 billion yuan, with billion-yuan firms taking 63% — and which numbers in self-purchase announcements are real signals.
