Tudor's $9bn Spinout: The Hard Part Isn't the Money

2026-11-28 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

Tudor's quant unit, $9 billion, 300 people, first half of 2027 — leaving as one block.

That is what Xantium plans to take out of Tudor Investment Corporation. The Financial Times reported it first on 1 November; Hedgeweek and HedgeCo followed. If it lands at that size it will be the largest hedge fund spinout on record, ahead of the $8.4bn Leda Braga took from BlueCrest in 2015 and the $5bn Diego Megia raised for Taula after leaving Millennium in 2024.

The record itself is not the story. The timing is. Independent launches are having their hardest year in a while — costs are up, and multi-strategy platforms are holding people with cash. This one is breaking away anyway.

How $5bn became $9bn

At the start of 2026 the estimate for the spinout was around $5bn. By November it was $9bn. Same team, same strategy, $4bn more in eight months.

We wrote about this firm in July. Back then the only hard fact was a Hong Kong subsidiary registration; the size was still a $5bn outside estimate, and there was no timetable. This time is different: the $9bn figure and the first-half 2027 date are both new.

That is not performance. That is clients queueing. One line in the reporting matters most: the bulk of the $9bn belongs to outside investors, with Tudor, management and staff supplying the rest. The money is already at the door. What is missing is a new entity to sign it over to.

Tudor is expected to back the firm in some form, but whether it keeps equity, and how much, is unresolved. Both firms declined to comment.

Read that carefully: the parent is still putting money in, so this is not a disposal; the equity question is open, so the terms are still on the table.

Three spinouts, three different things

Line the three records up and the difference is not size. It is what comes out.

YearFirm (parent)SizeWhat actually moved
2015Systematica (from BlueCrest)$8.4bnA team and its money; the parent was becoming a family office
2024Taula (from Millennium)$5bnA pod leader leaving on the back of a personal track record
2027Xantium (from Tudor)$9bn300 people, four offices, one multi-asset fund — the whole company

Systematica in 2015 was BlueCrest's shift to a family office model, with the quant team taking outside capital with it.

Taula in 2024 was a Millennium pod head going independent, with $5bn resting on a personal and team P&L record.

Xantium is different. It was incubated inside Tudor in 2019 and grew into a 300-person operation across four cities running a single multi-asset fund in equities, commodities, fixed income and FX, with systematic models carrying most of the portfolio risk. It is not a team taking money. It is a company taking a company.

Tudor has done this before. HedgeCo notes that Two Sigma was incubated at Tudor as well.

What actually blocks it

Money is the easy part. Three things are not.

First, every investor has to sign again. Units move from a strategy inside Tudor to a standalone company; the counterparty changes and the paperwork is redone. With most of the $9bn held by outside clients, that is a client-by-client process. The binding constraint on a reorganisation like this is never fundraising capacity — it is signature velocity.

Second, the infrastructure has to be cut apart. Codebase, data pipelines, execution algorithms, risk systems, backtest environments. None of it sits on the balance sheet, and all of it is the most valuable thing a quant firm owns. How it gets priced, licensed and maintained during the transition is the part that eats the calendar.

Third, equity has to be priced. If Tudor keeps a stake, the new firm's cap table carries a shareholder who is not in the business. If it does not, what does Tudor get for seven years of incubation and seed capital.

My own read is that size is the easiest piece here. $9bn looks dramatic, but it is already booked; nobody has to go on a roadshow for it. What decides whether this closes on schedule is those three pieces of paper.

The arithmetic of 300 people

$9bn across 300 people is $30m per head. That is not extreme for the industry, but watch the denominator: research, engineering, middle and back office, compliance and operations are all inside the 300. The people actually running money are a subset.

Of the four offices, Abu Dhabi deserves a second look. Gulf sovereign capital has been allocating into systematic strategies, and an office there works as both a fundraising point and a hiring point.

And Hong Kong? Bloomberg reported in July that Xantium registered a Hong Kong subsidiary in mid-May, with Asia led by Christina Ma, a 21-year Goldman Sachs veteran; Xantium declined to comment at the time. The FT's list of four offices does not include Hong Kong. Whether the territory becomes a fifth site, the firm has not said.

For the people inside

From announcement to a first-half 2027 close is roughly twelve months. In that window a 300-person organisation has to turn from a division into a company, and the list of roles it needs is fairly specific.

  • Standing up the entity. Compliance, a head of risk, finance, legal, HR — all shared inside the parent, all to be built from scratch outside it.
  • Switching the incentive structure. A standalone firm can offer equity and options; a platform cannot. A platform can pay high cash; a standalone firm usually cannot. At 300 people, the two rarely fit on one offer letter, and the transition is the real negotiation of the next year.
  • The scarce seats during migration. The binding hires around a spinout are not portfolio managers. They are the people who can take two systems apart and put them back together: infrastructure engineering, compliance, operations, risk. That window usually runs from announcement to day one.

Two things to watch

One: how much equity Tudor ultimately keeps. That number tells you whether this is genuine independence or a reorganisation under a new name.

Two: whether Xantium fills a chief risk officer seat before the first half of 2027. For a 300-person standalone firm, the first thing it must own is not a strategy. It is someone who can sign.
