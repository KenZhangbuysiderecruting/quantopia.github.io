China's Quant Top Tier Reset to ¥80B: 24 New ¥10B Firms in One Year

2026-10-15 · Ken Zhang / Gravitas Recruitment

The entry ticket to China's quant top table has moved from ¥10 billion to ¥80 billion. Caixin-affiliated Cailian reported on Oct 13 that, as of the end of Q3, six quant private funds now manage more than ¥80 billion: High-Flyer, Ubiquant, Minghong, Mingshi, Century Frontier and Yanfu. Mingshi and Century Frontier are new arrivals this quarter. That is only the apex. One layer down, the numbers get louder.

The full ladder

The ¥10B club added 24 firms in twelve months — 48% growth. Over the same stretch, total private-fund AUM in China went from ¥22.15tn to ¥25.75tn, up 16.25% (AMAC data through end-August). 48% against 16.25%: the direction of the money needs no adjective.

Two fuels: issuance and performance

Fuel one is issuance. In the first eight months of the year, 5,333 quant products were filed — 48.64% of all securities private-fund filings, close to half the market, up 40.86% year on year. The broader ¥10B club (all strategies) grew from 90 to 157 firms, +67 in a year; quant equity went from 35 to 56. Among the new ¥10B entrants, quant equity and discretionary long-only ran nearly neck and neck, 22 and 23 respectively.

Fuel two is performance. A Q3 review by SinoPac/Kaiyuan's quant team (relayed by Yiyi Trust and Tonghuashun; secondary media transmission, primary source not disclosed) puts private-fund CSI 500 index-enhancement excess return at 9.6% for Q3 against just 4.1% for public funds; CSI 1000 index-enhancement excess was 10.12% over the first three quarters versus 5.2% for public funds. After a choppy Q2, Q3 excess returns rebounded across the board to new highs.

The timing gap is the interesting part: excess returns peaked and money poured in during Q3 — then in the first week after the holiday, tech stocks sold off and some quant books lost more than 2.5% in a single week. Money arrives on last quarter's report card and is handed next quarter's exam paper.

Global mirror: concentration is not a China-only script

Zoom out and the same play is running everywhere. Millennium manages $97 billion (Bloomberg, Sept 8) and in October closed $22 billion of new subscriptions. Man Group, the largest listed hedge fund globally, passed $253.6 billion in AUM in its H1 results (July 28). China's ¥80 billion entry line converts to roughly $11.2 billion — about one-ninth of Millennium. The figures are not strictly comparable, of course: Man runs multi-asset, multi-strategy mandates and Millennium is a multi-strategy platform, neither of which is the same exam paper as a domestic quant private fund.

But the direction is identical. In New York, London or Shanghai, incremental capital is crowding into the platform players. Global hedge fund AUM is at a record while mid-sized firms generally have a harder time than the leaders.

This list is an org-chart progress bar

For anyone hiring, 74 quant firms at ¥10B+ versus 50 a year ago means 24 firms that each need more researchers, more IT and more execution capacity. The new entrants include Shenyi, Luoshu, Tengsheng and Hongxi, promoted out of the ¥5–10B tier, as well as firms like Huanian that were not even in the ¥5B+ cohort at the end of last year and jumped straight into the ¥10B club.

The other half of a scale race is capacity. A Tonghuashun statistical piece on Oct 15 flagged something routinely overlooked: ¥80 billion is client assets, not net worth. Money getting in is the easy part; whether the models can absorb it is the next exam. The observation I find more useful: among candidates I have spoken with over the past three months, interview invitations from newly minted ¥10B firms have noticeably thickened. Scale arrives first, teams catch up later — that lag is the hiring window right now.
